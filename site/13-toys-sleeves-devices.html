Chapter 13 — Toys, Sleeves & Devices#

Tools extend what hands can do: they deliver sensations (vibration, enveloping pressure, internal stimulation) that hands can’t, they free the hands for other zones, and they rest the hands during long sessions. They also introduce material safety, hygiene, and cost considerations. This chapter is a buyer’s and user’s guide organized by category, with a materials-safety primer first, because the material matters more than the brand.

We don’t recommend specific products or brands — models change, and this guide should stay useful. We describe categories, features, and what to look for.

13.1 Materials: the most important decision#

A toy touches mucosal or near-mucosal tissue, sometimes internally, for extended periods. What it’s made of determines whether it’s safe, whether it can be cleaned, and how long it lasts.

Body-safe, non-porous (can be fully sanitized)#

  • Platinum-cure silicone. The gold standard for soft toys. Non-porous, hypoallergenic, phthalate-free, temperature-responsive, can be boiled or bleached. Feels smooth-matte or slightly grippy. Ranges from very soft to firm. Downside: cost; and shouldn’t be used with silicone lube (surface degradation). Watch for: “silicone blend” or “silicone-like” — often a TPE mix. Pure silicone doesn’t have a strong chemical smell.
  • Borosilicate glass. Non-porous, hard, holds temperature (warm or chill it), essentially indestructible in normal use (drop-tested; check for chips before each use). Excellent for prostate toys and temperature play.
  • Stainless steel (surgical grade). Non-porous, heavy, holds temperature, firm pressure ideal for prostate. Expensive. Excellent.
  • ABS plastic. Hard, non-porous, common for vibrator bodies and bullet vibrators. Safe.
  • Aluminum (anodized). Non-porous; used in some premium toys. Fine.
  • Ceramic (glazed). Non-porous if the glaze is intact. Check for cracks.

Non-toxic but porous (cannot be fully sanitized; degrade over time)#

  • TPE / TPR (thermoplastic elastomer/rubber). The material of most strokers/sleeves (including the major brands’ “realistic” skin materials — all their proprietary names are TPE variants). Very soft, very realistic feel, inexpensive. Porous: bacteria and mold colonize the interior over time and can’t be fully removed. Requires meticulous drying and regular replacement. Not to be shared. Fine for external use by one person with good hygiene.
  • “Cyberskin” / “UR3” / “Real Feel” and similar. Soft TPE/PVC blends. Same story: porous, hard to dry, shorter lifespan, often oily. Frequent replacement.
  • Latex rubber. Porous; allergenic for some; degrades. Uncommon in quality toys now.
  • Elastomer blends (unspecified “rubber”). Assume porous.

Avoid#

  • Jelly / “jelly rubber” / soft PVC. Historically the cheapest toy material. Porous, often contains phthalates (plasticizers linked to endocrine disruption; a Danish EPA study found phthalates and other volatile compounds in a range of jelly/PVC toys), and often has a strong chemical smell. The smell is the tell. Not regulated as medical devices in most countries, so “phthalate-free” claims are unverified. Never internal. Ideally never at all.
  • Anything with a strong chemical or “shower curtain” smell.
  • “Novelty use only” labeling — a legal dodge that means the maker takes no responsibility for body contact.
  • Unlabeled materials from anonymous marketplace sellers.

The porous-toy rule#

If it’s porous (TPE, jelly, cyberskin), use it with a condom for internal use, dry it completely after every wash, store it dry, and replace it on a schedule (every 6–12 months for regular use, sooner if it discolors, smells, gets sticky, or develops tears). If you see dark spots inside a sleeve, that’s mold — throw it out.

Testing at home#

  • Smell. Body-safe silicone has almost no odor; TPE has a faint rubbery smell; jelly/PVC has a strong chemical smell.
  • The flame test (folk method): touching a lighter to silicone leaves white ash and no melting; TPE/PVC melts and smokes. Roughly indicative, not reliable, and ruins the spot you test. Don’t rely on it.
  • Price. Pure silicone toys are rarely very cheap. Very cheap “silicone” is usually a blend.
  • Buy from reputable retailers who publish materials and stand behind them.

13.2 Strokers and sleeves#

A tube or sleeve, usually TPE, that the penis moves inside. The largest category of men’s toys.

Types#

  • Open-ended sleeves. Short, penetrable from both ends. Cheap; easy to clean and dry; portable; less “enveloping.”
  • Closed-end sleeves in a case (the canister style). A long TPE sleeve inside a hard case with an end cap that adjusts suction. The most popular format. Most realistic; heavier; more cleaning effort; suction is a distinct sensation.
  • Textured vs. smooth. Internal textures range from realistic (ribs, bumps) to abstract. More texture = more sensation but also more to clean. Very intense textures can be too much for some men and are often best with an overlubed, slow approach.
  • Orifice styles. Realistic (modeled on anatomy) or non-representational. Purely preference.
  • Tightness. Varies. Tighter isn’t better — very tight sleeves reduce lube flow and increase friction.
  • Automatic strokers. Motorized; move the sleeve for you at adjustable speed/patterns. Hands-free. Some sync to video or partner devices. Higher cost, more maintenance. Sensation can become monotonous (machine rhythm doesn’t vary); the best have irregular patterns.
  • Vibrating strokers. Sleeve with an embedded motor. The vibration transfers through the TPE, muted.
  • Suction / “blowjob” machines. Combine sleeve motion with suction cycles. Distinct sensation.
  • Silicone strokers. Firmer, less realistic, but non-porous and indefinitely cleanable. Growing category.

Using them well#

  • Lube generously — much more than for hand use. Water-based (standard; silicone lube may degrade some TPE — check the maker). Keep a spray bottle of water nearby to reactivate water-based lube.
  • Warm it. Cold TPE is unpleasant. Soak the sleeve in warm water for 5 minutes beforehand (or use the maker’s warming rod). This is a large upgrade in sensation.
  • Slow. The instinct is to use a stroker fast. Try slow — one stroke per 2–3 seconds — and feel the texture pass over the corona.
  • Vary the grip on the case — loose and tight change the internal pressure.
  • Twist the sleeve as it moves.
  • Adjust suction (if there’s an end cap): more suction on the outstroke is a distinct sensation.
  • Thrust into it rather than moving it — different muscle engagement, closer to intercourse.
  • Combine with a hand on the perineum, scrotum, or a prostate toy.
  • For premature ejaculation training, a warm, lubricated sleeve is the closest solo simulation of intercourse. Use it for stop-start practice.
  • For delayed ejaculation / death-grip retraining, a sleeve provides moderate, enveloping pressure that’s hard to turn into a “death grip.”

Cleaning and care (TPE)#

  1. Remove sleeve from case. Rinse with warm (not hot — TPE deforms) water, flushing through both ends.
  2. Use a toy cleaner or very mild unscented soap. No soap for some brands — they say it degrades the material; follow the maker. Isopropyl alcohol (70%) rinse is used by some for disinfection; it dries the material somewhat over time.
  3. Rinse thoroughly.
  4. Dry completely. This is the critical step. Pat the outside; hang or prop upright so air flows through; use a fan; or use a dedicated drying stick. Takes hours. A sleeve put away damp grows mold.
  5. Once dry, dust lightly with cornstarch (not talc — talc is a respiratory and possible carcinogenic concern; not flour — feeds bacteria) to restore the soft texture. Some makers sell “renewal powder” — it’s cornstarch.
  6. Store in the case or a breathable bag, away from other toys (TPE reacts with other soft materials), out of heat.

Replace when: sticky, discolored, torn, smelly, or showing any dark spots.

13.3 Vibrators for men#

Vibration recruits Pacinian receptors that hands can’t stimulate efficiently (Chapter 11). Men are late adopters, largely due to marketing, but the sensation is categorically different and worth exploring.

Types#

  • Bullet vibrators. Small, cheap, versatile. Held against the frenulum, glans, shaft, perineum, nipples. The best starting point.
  • Wand vibrators. Large, powerful, rumbly (low-frequency, deep). Held against the shaft or perineum, or with the penis pressed between the wand and the abdomen. Very strong; can produce orgasm hands-free; can cause numbness with prolonged use — take breaks.
  • Penis-specific vibrators. Shaped to cradle the glans, ring the shaft, or wrap the frenulum. Often the “hands-free” category.
  • Vibrating rings. A stretchy ring with a motor, worn at the base. Mild sensation for the wearer; mostly a partner toy.
  • Vibrating prostate massagers. See 13.5.
  • Vibrating strokers. See 13.2.
  • Glans-cup vibrators. Cup-shaped, sit over the head. Intense; often with multiple patterns.

What matters#

  • Rumbly vs. buzzy. Low-frequency, high-amplitude (“rumbly”) vibration penetrates deeper and feels richer. High-frequency, low-amplitude (“buzzy”) feels surface-level and causes numbness faster. Rumbly is generally better for men. Look for reviews using these terms.
  • Patterns. Steady, pulsing, escalating, random. Random/irregular patterns fight habituation.
  • Power range. You want the low end to be genuinely low (the frenulum is sensitive) and the high end to be strong.
  • Waterproofing. Useful for cleaning and shower use.
  • Rechargeable vs. battery. Rechargeable is better long-term.
  • Noise. Varies enormously; matters for privacy.
  • Material. Silicone or ABS. Nothing else.

Using them well#

  • Start low, on the frenulum, with a light touch — not pressed hard. Move it. A stationary vibrator on one spot causes numbness and habituation within a minute or two.
  • Through fabric or a hand to mute it if too intense.
  • On the perineum during penile stimulation — one of the best combinations.
  • On the shaft base / pubic bone — deep vibration transmits into the roots.
  • On the scrotum — gently; many men love this.
  • Nipples.
  • Hands-free orgasm: a wand or cradle-style vibrator held in place (pillow, thighs) on the frenulum/underside of the glans, with no stroking. The orgasm from pure vibration is different — often described as building slower, then very intense, with more involuntary contraction. Some men find it too intense; some find it the best thing they’ve tried.
  • Numbness: vibrators cause transient numbness with prolonged direct use — this is normal and resolves in minutes to an hour. If it persists, reduce intensity and duration. Chronic heavy vibrator use can raise the stimulation threshold (a variant of the conditioning issue); rotate with hand sessions.

13.4 Rings (constriction rings, “cock rings”)#

A ring worn around the base of the penis (and sometimes behind the scrotum too) that restricts venous outflow, making the erection firmer, fuller, and longer-lasting, and increasing glans sensitivity. Also delays ejaculation for some men and intensifies orgasm for others (the ring compresses the urethra, which changes the sensation of ejaculation).

Types#

  • Stretchy (silicone, TPE). Safe for beginners. Can always be stretched off.
  • Adjustable (lasso/snap style). Adjustable tightness with a quick release.
  • Rigid (metal, glass, hard plastic). Firmer sensation; the most “effective”; also the ones that get stuck. For experienced users who know their size (measure the flaccid circumference at the base, add a little — a rigid ring must go on flaccid or semi-erect and come off flaccid).
  • Vibrating rings. Covered above.
  • Ring + ball strap combos. Around the shaft and behind the scrotum. More restriction; more to go wrong.

Rules (non-negotiable)#

  • Maximum 20–30 minutes. Set a timer.
  • Remove immediately at pain, numbness, coldness, purple/blue color, or if the erection becomes uncomfortably hard.
  • Never sleep with one on. Never wear under alcohol or sedation (you won’t notice the warning signs).
  • Never a rigid ring you haven’t sized. Stuck rigid rings require emergency departments and tools.
  • Lube the ring for application and removal.
  • Start with stretchy. Rigid rings are a later step, if at all.

Sensation#

Many men find rings enhance solo sessions modestly — firmer erection, more sensitive glans, a “full” feeling. Others find them uncomfortable or that they blunt the orgasm. Try a cheap stretchy one before investing.

13.5 Prostate massagers and anal toys#

Types#

  • Prostate massagers (contoured). Shaped with a curved shaft, a bulbous head to reach the prostate, a narrow neck for the sphincter to grip, a flared base with a perineum arm that presses the external spot. Designed to be held in place by the pelvic floor and worked hands-free by contracting/releasing. The classic design. Sizes from small (beginner) to large.
  • Vibrating prostate massagers. Same shape plus motors — sometimes in the head, the base, and the perineum arm. Remote-controlled options exist. Vibration on the prostate is a distinct sensation that many men find far easier to build arousal from than static pressure.
  • “Come-hither” / stroking massagers. Motorized head that moves in a beckoning motion, mimicking a finger. Newer category; strong reports.
  • Butt plugs. Fullness without specific prostate targeting. Useful for beginners to adapt to the sensation, and during penile stimulation for the added dimension.
  • Anal beads. Graduated spheres; the sensation is in insertion and especially removal at orgasm.
  • Dildos (curved). For men who prefer motion to static pressure; needs a hand.
  • Glass and steel prostate toys. Firm, heavy, temperature-holding. Excellent for firm, precise pressure. Not for beginners due to firmness.
  • Inflatable plugs. Adjustable fullness. Advanced.

Choosing your first#

  • Small. Diameter 2.5–3 cm (1–1.2 in) at the widest. Beginners overestimate.
  • Silicone. Non-porous, some flex.
  • Flared base, obviously.
  • Contoured prostate design if prostate pleasure is the goal; small plug if adaptation is the goal.
  • Vibration is optional but helpful for beginners — it “shows” you where the prostate is.
  • Budget $30–80 for a good silicone beginner massager. Expensive premium models are for later.

Using them#

Everything in Chapter 5.7 and 12.1 applies. Specifically:

  • Arousal 7+ before insertion. Heavily lubricated toy and opening. Slow. Press, wait for the sphincter to release on an exhale, ease in. Once past the widest point, the neck sits in the sphincter and the toy stays.
  • Lie on your side with knees drawn up (easiest insertion), then move to your back or knees.
  • Pelvic floor contractions move the head against the prostate. Rhythmic contract-release is the primary “technique.” Add hip rocking.
  • Don’t pull it in and out rapidly at first — the sensation is about pressure and pelvic floor movement, not thrusting.
  • Combine with penile stimulation for the first many sessions.
  • Sessions of 20–45 minutes for prostate development.
  • Remove slowly, on an exhale, relaxed.
  • Wash immediately with warm water and soap (silicone) or boil/bleach-solution periodically.

13.6 Other categories#

  • Penis pumps (vacuum). Medical (ED) or pleasure. Use with a gauge and limit; 10–15 minutes; stop at pain or dark discoloration. Produces a temporary swollen, hypersensitive state some men enjoy. See Chapter 12.5.
  • Nipple toys. Clamps (adjustable, start loose), suction cups, small vibrators. Nipple sensitivity develops with use.
  • Textured gloves and touch tools. Fleece, silk, faux-fur mitts, soft brushes, feather ticklers, Wartenberg pinwheels (light, gently). Cheap, low-tech, and effective for sensory layering (Chapter 11).
  • Temperature tools. Glass and steel toys warmed or chilled; warm damp cloths; ice (briefly, never directly on the glans for long). A hot-water bottle on the abdomen.
  • Massage oils and candles (low-temp massage candles) for non-genital warm-up.
  • Blindfolds, headphones. Sensory reduction.
  • Restraints (solo). Limited utility alone and a real safety concern — never anything you can’t release instantly with one hand.
  • Masturbation “machines” / sex machines. Thrusting devices for anal use or with a sleeve. Expensive; niche; follow safety instructions.
  • Interactive / teledildonic devices. Sync with content or a partner remotely. Data privacy concerns (Chapter 20).
  • Penis extenders / traction devices. Medical use for Peyronie’s; consumer use for “enlargement” — modest gains in length (~1–2 cm) are documented with months of daily traction [Nikoobakht 2011 and others], with significant time commitment and some risk. Not a pleasure device; mentioned for completeness.
  • Ball stretchers / weights. Not recommended (Chapter 12.5).
  • Sounds. Not recommended (Chapter 12.5).

13.7 Hygiene and storage#

  • Clean before and after every use. Non-porous: soap + warm water; periodically boil (silicone, glass, steel — check motors aren’t in the boiled part) or soak in 10% bleach solution then rinse thoroughly. Porous: warm water + toy cleaner; dry completely; replace on schedule.
  • Dry fully before storage. Moisture is the enemy.
  • Store separately. Different soft materials can react and degrade each other when touching (especially TPE against TPE or silicone). Individual cloth bags.
  • Away from heat and sunlight.
  • Check before each use for tears, cracks, sticky surfaces, discoloration, odor. Discard damaged toys.
  • Don’t share porous toys, ever. Non-porous toys can be shared with a condom or after full disinfection.
  • Lube compatibility: water-based with everything; silicone lube not on silicone toys; oil not on TPE or latex.

13.8 Buying guide summary#

Goal First purchase Later
Different sensation than hand Bullet vibrator (silicone/ABS) Wand; glans cup
Intercourse-like feel; PE training; DE retraining Open-ended TPE sleeve or canister stroker Automatic stroker; silicone stroker

Where to buy: reputable specialty retailers (online or physical) that list materials, have return policies, and don’t sell jelly/PVC. Avoid anonymous marketplace sellers for anything internal or soft.

What to spend: a good beginner kit — bullet vibe, stretchy ring, open sleeve, small prostate massager, body-safe lube — runs $100–200. You don’t need more to explore every category in this chapter.

Key takeaways#

  • Material first. Silicone, glass, steel, ABS = non-porous, sanitizable, safe. TPE = porous, fine for external solo use with strict drying and replacement. Jelly/PVC = avoid.
  • Strokers: lube generously, warm them, go slow, dry completely after washing, cornstarch to restore, replace when degraded.
  • Vibrators: choose rumbly over buzzy; start low on the frenulum; keep it moving; use on the perineum and scrotum; take breaks to avoid numbness.
  • Rings: stretchy first; 20–30 minutes max; remove at any warning sign; never sleep in one; never an unsized rigid ring.
  • Prostate massagers: small, silicone, flared, contoured; arousal 7+ before insertion; pelvic floor contractions do the work; combine with penile stimulation.
  • Clean, dry, store separately, inspect, replace. Don’t share porous toys.
