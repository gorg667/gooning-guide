Chapter 12 — Advanced Sensations: Prostate, Perineum, Non-Ejaculatory & Multiple Orgasms#

This chapter covers the practices that extend male sexual experience beyond the standard penile-stroke-to-ejaculation pattern: prostate stimulation, non-ejaculatory orgasm, multiple orgasms, and related techniques. It also covers a few practices (urethral sounding, extreme constriction) that circulate online and carry real risk.

Evidence quality here is lower than in earlier chapters. Prostate orgasms and male multiple orgasms are documented in clinical case literature and sexology surveys but have been studied far less than the standard response cycle. Much of what’s known comes from practitioner communities. We flag the evidence level throughout and separate “well-documented” from “widely reported” from “speculative.”

12.1 The prostate#

What’s known#

  • The prostate is richly innervated by the pelvic plexus and responds to pressure with sensation ranging from neutral to intensely pleasurable [Levin 2018]. 🟢
  • Prostate stimulation can produce orgasm, with or without ejaculation. Levin’s review describes prostate-induced orgasms as reported to be “more intense, more diffuse, longer-lasting, and associated with greater feelings of ecstasy” than penile orgasms, and notes they can occur repeatedly without a refractory period. 🟡 (clinical reviews and surveys; no controlled physiological studies)
  • Most men report no pleasure — or discomfort — from prostate stimulation at first, and increasing pleasure with repeated practice. Levin proposes a learning/neuroplastic mechanism: the brain learns to interpret the novel signal as sexual. 🟡
  • The time to develop prostate pleasure varies enormously: some men within a few sessions, others after months, some never. 🟢 (consistent across all sources)
  • Prostate stimulation does not indicate anything about sexual orientation. The prostate is innervated identically regardless of who you’re attracted to. 🟢

Anatomy recap#

5–7 cm (2–3 inches) inside the rectum, toward the front of the body (toward the pubic bone). Walnut-sized, firm but yielding, with a shallow groove down the middle. When aroused, it swells and becomes easier to locate. The seminal vesicles are just above and behind it. The membranous urethra runs just below it — pressure there produces a distinct “need to urinate” or “about to ejaculate” sensation.

Prerequisites#

  • Chapter 5.7 (anal hygiene and safety) — read it.
  • Trimmed nails or a glove/finger cot. Lots of lube (low-osmolality water-based or silicone). Never numbing lube.
  • Arousal first. Get to 6–7 with penile stimulation before any anal touch. The anus relaxes with arousal; the prostate engorges with arousal; and the brain is primed to read new signals as sexual.
  • Relaxation. The external sphincter needs time. Slow breath, reverse Kegels.
  • Patience across sessions. This is a weeks-to-months project, not a single-session goal.

Progression#

Stage 1 — External (several sessions). Lubricated fingertip circling the anal opening while stroking the penis. Light pressure on the opening without entering. Perineal pressure. The goal: the nervous system learns that this area is part of sex. Many men find this alone adds a distinct layer and stop here. That’s fine.

Stage 2 — Entry (several sessions). At arousal 7, with a heavily lubricated finger, press gently against the opening and wait — don’t push. On an exhale and reverse Kegel, the sphincter opens slightly; let the finger slide in to the first knuckle. Hold still. Breathe. When comfortable, further. Continue penile stimulation throughout (or pause and resume — some men find combining them overwhelming at first). The goal: comfort with the sensation of fullness and the sphincter stretch. Don’t seek the prostate yet.

Stage 3 — Locating. Finger in to the second knuckle or beyond, curling toward the front (toward the pubic bone, as if pointing at the base of the penis from inside). You’ll feel a firm, rounded bulge — the prostate — through the rectal wall. Press gently. The sensation at first is often odd: a dull pressure, a need-to-urinate feeling, maybe nothing. That’s expected. Gentle circular pressure, or a slow “come here” beckoning motion with the fingertip.

Stage 4 — Combining. Prostate pressure while stroking the penis. The most common breakthrough: the prostate sensation that felt “odd” alone becomes distinctly pleasurable when penile arousal is at 8+. Press firmly on the prostate at the edge, and during orgasm. Many men who felt nothing from the prostate alone discover that prostate pressure during a penile orgasm dramatically intensifies it. This is the reliable, accessible version of prostate pleasure, and for many men it’s the destination.

Stage 5 — Prostate-focused (weeks to months). Reduce penile stimulation; let prostate stimulation carry more of the arousal. A toy (Chapter 13) is much easier than a finger for this — fingers tire, angles are awkward, and a well-shaped prostate massager held in place by the pelvic floor can be worked hands-free. Contract and release the pelvic floor around the toy rhythmically. Small rocking movements of the hips. Breath. Attention entirely on the internal sensation. The commonly reported experience is a slow build over 20–60 minutes: warmth, fullness, pleasant pressure, then waves of sensation that feel like the beginning of an orgasm that don’t peak in the usual way, then, with practice, waves that do peak — the “prostate orgasm” — without ejaculation and without refractory period. Some men reach this in a few weeks; many take months; some never do, and enjoy the intermediate states.

What a prostate orgasm is like (as reported)#

Compiled from clinical descriptions and community reports; individual experience varies widely:

  • Builds slowly rather than sharply; often no clear “point of no return.”
  • Felt deep in the pelvis and spreading outward, rather than in the penis.
  • Often described as waves — sometimes several minutes of rolling peaks.
  • Frequently involves involuntary muscle contractions, trembling, vocalization.
  • Erection may be present or absent; it’s not required.
  • Usually no ejaculation, though prostatic fluid may leak (“milking”). Some men ejaculate.
  • Little or no refractory period; can be repeated or followed by a penile orgasm.
  • Many men describe it as more emotional or “whole-body” than penile orgasm.

Troubleshooting#

  • “I feel nothing.” Normal for weeks. Keep combining with penile arousal at 8+. Try more pressure (the prostate tolerates firm pressure), or less. Try a toy with a different shape.
  • “It just feels like I need to pee.” You’re pressing on the urethra/bladder area — aim slightly higher or more to the side. Also, empty the bladder beforehand. The sensation usually transforms with continued practice.
  • “It’s uncomfortable.” Slow down. More lube. More arousal before entry. Smaller toy. Never push through pain.
  • “I lose my erection.” Common and irrelevant to prostate pleasure. Some men’s erections soften during anal play; the pleasure is separate.
  • “I keep tensing up.” Reverse Kegels. Long exhales. Try a toy instead of a finger so both hands and attention are free.
  • “I got close but didn’t peak.” The characteristic plateau. Keep practicing. Trying to force it inhibits it (prefrontal effect). Let it build without goal.
  • “There’s a small amount of clear fluid.” Prostatic fluid expressed by pressure. Normal.
  • “Blood.” Stop. Minor fissure; wait until healed. Persistent bleeding: doctor.

Milking#

“Prostate milking” is the expression of prostatic fluid via massage, with or without orgasm. Historically used medically for prostatitis (efficacy debated). As a sexual practice it overlaps with the above. Not a health intervention.

12.2 The perineum and the “external prostate”#

Firm upward pressure midway between scrotum and anus compresses the bulb of the penis, the membranous urethra, and reaches toward the prostate. This is accessible to everyone, requires nothing, and reliably intensifies orgasm for most men who try it.

  • During plateau: rhythmic pressure with fingertips or knuckles, synchronized with strokes.
  • At the edge: firm sustained pressure can either drop arousal (by interrupting the ejaculatory reflex) or spike it — depends on the man. Learn which for you.
  • During orgasm: pressure in time with the contractions. The most common single technique for intensifying penile orgasm.
  • The “hold-back” (not recommended): pressing very hard on the perineum at ejaculation to block semen from exiting. It works — semen is forced back into the bladder (retrograde ejaculation) — and the orgasm continues without visible ejaculate. It’s a Taoist “injaculation” practice. Risks: retrograde ejaculation can, with repeated practice, become habitual; forceful pressure on the membranous urethra can cause irritation or, rarely, injury; and there’s no evidence of the claimed “energy conservation” benefits. Occasional use is unlikely to harm; regular use is not advised.

12.3 Non-ejaculatory orgasm (NEO) and multiple orgasms#

What’s known#

  • Orgasm and ejaculation are neurologically separable (Chapter 3). 🟢
  • Men can learn to orgasm without ejaculating; this is reported in sexological literature since Kinsey and documented in case reports and surveys [Dunn & Trost 1989; Whipple, Myers & Komisaruk 1998 — a case study of a man with verified multiple orgasms]. 🟡
  • Non-ejaculatory orgasm produces little or no refractory period, permitting multiple orgasms in a session. 🟡
  • The proportion of men who can learn this is unknown. Practitioner communities suggest most men can learn some version with months of practice; a minority learn it quickly; some never do. 🟠
  • Prolactin, the prime refractory-period suspect, surges after ejaculation; it’s unclear whether it surges after non-ejaculatory orgasm. Small studies suggest less or none [Krüger 2003, indirectly]. 🟠

Three routes#

Route 1 — The prostate route. Described above. Prostate orgasms are typically non-ejaculatory by nature. This is the most reliable route for men who develop prostate pleasure.

Route 2 — The edging route (the “dry orgasm” or “mini-orgasm”). With extensive edging practice, many men begin to experience, at 9–9.5, a brief wave of orgasmic sensation without tipping into ejaculation — a few involuntary pelvic contractions, a rush, and then arousal drops to 8 rather than to zero. This is often called a mini-orgasm, dry orgasm, or “peak.” It emerges naturally from long-term edging in many men; it’s not forced. Techniques that encourage it:

  • Hold at 9 (not 9.5) longer and longer, with complete body relaxation and slow breath.
  • At the first sign of involuntary pelvic pulses, stop all stimulation and relax the pelvic floor completely (reverse Kegel). Do not clench. Let whatever happens happen.
  • Sometimes the result is a ruined orgasm (ejaculation, no peak); sometimes nothing; sometimes — increasingly, with practice — a peak with no ejaculation. The distinction is where on the 9–9.5 boundary you were.
  • Once a dry orgasm happens, arousal drops to 7–8; resume stimulation; build again; another peak may be available. Sequences of 3–10 peaks over a session are reported by experienced practitioners. 🟠

Route 3 — The clench (the “Kegel block”). At 9.5 — the point of no return, as emission begins — a maximum-force sustained pelvic floor contraction held for 10–15 seconds. Orgasm proceeds (the contractions are felt), but the strong voluntary contraction of the bulbospongiosus and external urethral sphincter partially or fully blocks expulsion. Semen may be retained in the urethra (and dribble out later), may go retrograde into the bladder, or may be partially expelled. Results reported: orgasm sensation usually present, sometimes muted; refractory period reduced but often not eliminated (since emission occurred); second orgasm possible within minutes. This is the technique in The Multi-Orgasmic Man (Chia & Arava 1996) and many online guides. 🟠

Caveats for Route 3: it requires a strong, well-trained pelvic floor (Chapter 10, several weeks in); timing is very precise and mistimed attempts either don’t block anything or produce a ruined orgasm; frequent retrograde ejaculation may cause some men bladder irritation; and the technique tends to produce a “blocked” or “incomplete” sensation for many men rather than the “full orgasm, no ejaculation” it promises. Routes 1 and 2 produce more satisfying non-ejaculatory orgasms according to most practitioners. Route 3 is included because it’s widely taught, it does work for some men, and knowing its limitations is useful.

Multiple ejaculatory orgasms#

Separately: some men, especially younger, can ejaculate a second (or third) time within minutes. The refractory period is short or absent for them at high arousal. Continued or renewed stimulation immediately after the first orgasm — through the hypersensitive phase, which is uncomfortable for many — leads to a second ejaculation, typically smaller and less intense. This is not learnable in the same way; it’s largely constitutional and declines with age. If it’s available to you, enjoy it; if not, the non-ejaculatory routes are the trainable path to multiples.

A realistic timeline#

  • Weeks 1–4: edging proficiency (Chapter 9); pelvic floor foundations (Chapter 10). Nothing else yet.
  • Months 2–3: holding at 9 for extended periods; first mini-peaks may appear; prostate exploration if interested.
  • Months 3–6: dry orgasms with some reliability for those in whom they develop; prostate pleasure developing.
  • Months 6–12+: multiple non-ejaculatory orgasms per session for some; prostate orgasms for some.

Some men are much faster; some plateau. Treat it as an interesting practice with pleasant intermediate states, not a pass/fail.

12.4 Combining everything: the extended-orgasm session#

For men who’ve developed the components:

  1. Standard build and edging (Chapter 9, 11) to 8–9.
  2. Prostate toy in place from arousal 7.
  3. At each edge: reverse Kegel, full relaxation, breath. Allow dry peaks if they come. Arousal drops to 8; continue.
  4. Prostate stimulation carries arousal during pauses in penile touch.
  5. Over 30–60 minutes: a series of peaks, waves, and prostate sensations.
  6. Finish with an ejaculatory orgasm with perineal/prostate pressure — reported as exceptionally intense after this kind of session — or don’t, and let the session end without ejaculation.

12.5 Other practices: what circulates online, and the risks#

Urethral sounding#

Inserting a smooth rod (“sound”) into the urethra. Real practice with a real following. Real risks: urethral tears, infection (UTI, and introduction of bacteria into the prostate — prostatitis), urethral stricture (scarring that permanently narrows the urethra, affecting urination and ejaculation), and — with objects not designed for it — foreign bodies requiring surgical removal. If you’re going to do it despite this: only surgical stainless steel or medical silicone sounds designed for the purpose; sterilized (not just washed); sterile lube; never force; never past resistance; stop at any pain or bleeding; never with anything that can break or be lost. Urologists see the failures. We don’t recommend it; we’d rather you know the harm-reduction facts than not.

Extreme constriction / prolonged rings#

Rings are covered in Chapter 13 — 20–30 minutes maximum, quick-release or stretch, remove at any pain, numbness, cold, or color change. “Sleeping with a ring on,” rigid metal rings that get stuck, or multiple stacked rings for hours are how men end up in emergency departments having rings cut off with power tools. Ischemic damage can be permanent.

Vacuum pumps#

Legitimate medical devices (for ED) and legitimate pleasure devices, with a real risk profile if misused: bruising, petechiae, lymphatic swelling, numbness, and with extreme or prolonged vacuum, tissue damage. Use a pump with a pressure gauge and release valve; stay well under the maximum; 10–15 minutes per session; stop at pain. “Pumping” for size increase produces temporary swelling (edema), not growth, and repeated over-pumping damages the erectile tissue.

Ball stretching, weights, and testicular pressure#

Some men enjoy gentle downward traction on the scrotum (it’s a legitimate sensation and a delay technique). Hanging weights, prolonged stretching devices, and forceful compression risk testicular trauma, torsion, and nerve damage. Gentle, brief, hand-only traction is fine. Devices are not recommended.

Electrostimulation (e-stim)#

Devices designed for the purpose (not TENS units repurposed, and never anything above the waist, which risks cardiac arrhythmia) deliver current through electrodes on the penis, perineum, or via insertable probes. Enthusiasts report unique sensations and hands-free orgasms. Use only purpose-built devices from reputable makers, follow instructions exactly, never near the heart, never if you have a pacemaker or heart condition, and start at minimum settings.

Breath play, choking, and “autoerotic asphyxiation”#

Do not. This kills several hundred people per year. There’s no safe way to do it alone; the mechanism (hypoxia) is the same one that causes unconsciousness, and unconsciousness while alone with a ligature or bag is fatal. Any “safety” device or method can fail. This is the one practice in this guide with a flat prohibition rather than harm reduction.

Chemsex / substance-enhanced solo sex#

Chapter 18 covers substances. Briefly: alcohol impairs; cannabis at low doses enhances for many and impairs at higher doses; poppers (alkyl nitrites) relax smooth muscle including the anal sphincter and produce a brief rush, with real risks (blood pressure drop, dangerous with PDE5 inhibitors, chemical burns if spilled, and a form of retinal damage with some formulations); stimulants (meth, cocaine, MDMA) produce intense wanting with impaired liking and function, high compulsivity risk, and serious harms. Not recommended.

Key takeaways#

  • The prostate is a legitimate, learnable, orientation-irrelevant source of intense pleasure. It typically takes weeks to months to develop; start with external touch, combine with penile arousal at 8+, then progress to focused stimulation with a toy. Most men’s first breakthrough is prostate pressure during a penile orgasm.
  • Perineal pressure timed to orgasmic contractions is the simplest reliable intensifier.
  • Non-ejaculatory orgasm is real and has three routes: prostate, edging-derived dry peaks, and the clench. The first two produce better results for most men. Expect months, not sessions.
  • Multiple orgasms follow from non-ejaculatory orgasms (little refractory period).
  • Sounding, prolonged constriction, ball weights, over-pumping carry real injury risk. Breath play alone is lethal. No exceptions.
