Chapter 6 — Fundamentals of Technique#

Most men have one technique: the one they discovered at 13, performed with the dominant hand, at one speed, with one grip, until it works. This chapter dismantles that and rebuilds it as a repertoire. Every element — grip, stroke, speed, pressure, hand position, what part of the penis gets stimulated, whether you’re dry or lubricated — is a variable you can control. Mastery is knowing the variables and choosing among them.

Read Chapter 2 (anatomy) first if you haven’t: technique is applied anatomy.

6.1 The variables#

Every stroke has these dimensions:

Variable Range What it changes
Grip type Full fist, thumb-and-fingers ring, two fingers, palm, fingertips Which surfaces are contacted and how much of the shaft
Pressure Barely touching → firm Depth of sensation (skin vs. erectile bodies), speed of arousal, risk of desensitization
Speed Glacial (one stroke per several seconds) → very fast (several per second) Arousal rate, ability to stay aware, character of orgasm
Stroke length Glans-only, upper shaft, full length, base-only Which nerve zones are recruited
Direction/motion Up-down, twist, circular, tap, squeeze-release, static hold Type of stimulation (friction, pressure, stretch, vibration)
Hand Dominant, non-dominant, both Novelty, coordination, different pressure/angle
Position Lying supine, sitting, standing, kneeling, prone, side-lying Blood flow, pelvic floor engagement, accessibility of other zones
Lubrication Dry, light, generous Friction vs. glide; sensation quality; skin safety
Focus zone Frenulum, corona, glans, shaft, base, scrotum, perineum, everything Intensity and location of sensation
Rhythm Steady, accelerating, wave-like, irregular, paused Predictability (habituation) vs. surprise

You don’t need to think about all ten at once. But recognizing that each one exists — and that changing any of them changes the experience — is the foundation.

6.2 Grips#

6.2.1 The full grip (fist)#

The default for most men. All four fingers wrap the shaft, thumb closes the circle, palm contacts the underside or top depending on hand orientation.

  • Overhand (palm toward the body, thumb on top or on the frenulum side): the most common. The thumb rides over the frenulum/glans on the upstroke — often the main source of sensation.
  • Underhand (palm facing away, thumb on the underside): less common, rotates which fingers contact which surface, changes the angle. Worth trying — many men find it distinctly different.
  • Pressure control: this grip makes it easy to squeeze too hard. Calibrate: hold your erect penis with a grip just firm enough that the skin moves with your hand rather than your hand sliding over it. That’s the maximum for a lubricated full stroke; you should spend much of your time lighter than that.

6.2.2 The ring (thumb and index/middle fingers)#

An “OK” sign around the shaft. Contacts a narrow band, allowing precise placement — over the corona, over the frenulum, at the base.

  • Excellent for corona-focused stimulation: the ring passes back and forth over the ridge.
  • Lower total pressure than a fist; harder to over-grip.
  • Pairs well with the other hand doing something else.

6.2.3 Two-finger / fingertip techniques#

Index and middle finger (or just the pads of fingers) on a specific spot — usually the frenulum or the underside of the glans.

  • Frenulum rub: lubricated fingertip(s) moving in small circles or short strokes on the frenulum. One of the highest-sensation-per-effort techniques available. Can bring a man from 5 to 9 quickly; use for pushing arousal or for finishing.
  • Glans polish: lubricated palm or fingers moving over the head. Intense, borderline overwhelming for many men — use light pressure and lots of lube. Some men can’t tolerate it; some find it their favorite thing.
  • Underside stroke: fingers pressing gently along the raphe/corpus spongiosum from base to frenulum. Deeper, less sharp sensation.

6.2.4 The palm#

Flat palm against the underside, penis pressed against the abdomen, moving up and down or in circles. Broad, diffuse stimulation. Different from any gripping technique because it’s pressure and warmth over a large area rather than friction on a band. Good for slow, low-arousal phases.

6.2.5 Two-handed techniques#

  • Stacked hands: both hands wrapped one above the other, moving together — essentially a longer grip, contacting the full shaft.
  • Alternating: one hand strokes up and releases as the other begins at the base, creating a continuous upward (or downward) motion with no return stroke. Feels quite different from a single hand.
  • Base hold + stroke: one hand holds the base firmly (mild constriction, increasing glans engorgement and giving a sense of “fullness”), the other strokes the upper shaft/glans.
  • Stroke + secondary zone: one hand on the penis, the other on the scrotum, perineum, nipples, inner thigh, or anus. This is the single most underused category of technique (Chapter 11).
  • Twist: one hand strokes while rotating around the shaft; or both hands rotate in opposite directions (gently).

6.2.6 No-hands#

  • Thrusting into a sleeve or pillow — recruits the hips and pelvic floor as in intercourse. Different muscle engagement, different sensation, useful for training thrusting stamina.
  • Prone grinding — against a mattress. See Chapter 5’s cautions (perineal pressure, and it’s a known contributor to delayed ejaculation if it’s the exclusive method). Fine as one item in a repertoire.
  • Vibration — Chapter 13.

6.3 Strokes and motions#

6.3.1 The linear stroke#

Up and down along the shaft. The universal default. Variables within it:

  • Full-length: base to glans and back. Recruits all zones.
  • Upper-half: focuses on corona and glans. Higher intensity per stroke.
  • Lower-half / base: shaft-only, avoids the glans. Lower intensity, good for sustaining arousal at a plateau without approaching orgasm. Also good if the glans is oversensitive.
  • Skin-moving vs. skin-sliding: in uncircumcised men (and circumcised men with enough loose skin), the hand can move the skin over the underlying shaft — the “gliding” action. Or with lube, the hand can slide over stationary skin. These are distinct sensations. Gliding produces stretch on the frenulum and corona; sliding produces friction on the surface. Try both.

6.3.2 The twist#

Hand rotates around the shaft as it moves, so the palm/fingers spiral. Especially intense as the twist passes over the corona. Common in sleeves with internal texture for this reason. Slow twists are one of the best techniques for extending plateau without tipping over.

6.3.3 Circular / glans-focused#

Palm or fingers moving in circles over the head, lubricated. Intense. Best in small doses or with very light pressure.

6.3.4 Tapping and percussion#

Fingers lightly tapping the glans, frenulum, or shaft. Novel, produces a fluttering sensation. Can be surprisingly effective for men who’ve habituated to stroking.

6.3.5 Squeeze-and-release (pulsing)#

Hand stationary, rhythmically squeezing and releasing the shaft. No friction. Stimulates via pressure on the erectile bodies. Excellent for holding at a plateau, and for men whose skin needs a break from friction.

6.3.6 Static hold#

Simply holding the erect penis, still, firmly or lightly, while attention rests on the sensation — often combined with pelvic floor contractions (which move the penis within the hand) or breathing. Sounds like nothing; it isn’t. Used in edging to let arousal subside without losing contact, and in mindfulness practice.

6.3.7 Stretch and traction#

Gently pulling the penis away from the body (by the shaft, not the glans) and holding, or pulling the skin down toward the base to tension the frenulum. Distinct, deep sensation. Do not do this forcefully or at full erection with a tight frenulum.

6.3.8 The “milking” stroke#

A single direction only — e.g., base to tip, release, repeat — using alternating hands. Emphasizes anticipation because each stroke is a complete event. Also useful post-ejaculation to express remaining semen (some men find this pleasurable or unpleasant — individual).

6.4 Speed#

Speed is the variable most men use most and understand least.

  • Fast (2–4+ strokes/second): raises arousal quickly; narrows attention; often produces a shorter, sharper orgasm; hard to stay aware of arousal level (you blow past 9 before you notice); encourages tight grip; skin-punishing without lube. Appropriate for the final approach to orgasm, or for a deliberately quick session. Inappropriate as a default for anything else.
  • Moderate (about 1 stroke/second): sustainable; good arousal ramp; aware of where you are.
  • Slow (one stroke every 2–5 seconds): this is where most of the pleasure lives, and where most men never spend time. Each stroke is felt as a complete sensation with a beginning, middle, and end. Arousal climbs slowly and can be held at a plateau. Orgasm reached after a long slow build is typically longer and more intense.
  • Glacial (one stroke per 10+ seconds): a mindfulness practice as much as a technique. Every millimeter of movement is noticed. Used in the “extreme slow” exercise (Chapter 11).

The general principle: speed should be inversely related to how much of the session remains. Start slow, stay slow through most of it, accelerate only when you’ve decided to finish — and even then, many men find that a slow approach to orgasm produces a better orgasm than a fast one.

6.5 Pressure#

  • Calibration exercise. Erect, lubricated, full grip. Squeeze as hard as you’d usually squeeze. Note it. Now halve the pressure. Stroke for one minute. Note the sensation — it’s probably less intense but more detailed; you feel more distinct zones. Now halve it again — barely touching. Stroke for one minute. This third level is where men who’ve never tried it discover they’ve been missing the entire surface of their skin.
  • Pressure and zone. The glans and frenulum want light pressure. The shaft tolerates and often prefers moderate pressure. The base can take firm pressure.
  • Pressure and arousal. Firmer pressure raises arousal faster but with less nuance. As a rule: light pressure to explore and extend, moderate pressure to build, firm pressure only briefly and only for finishing.
  • The tight-grip trap. If you need a tight grip to feel anything, that’s conditioning (Chapter 5, 16), not your anatomy. Two to four weeks of deliberately lighter, lubricated technique recalibrates it.

6.6 Dry vs. lubricated#

Dry masturbation relies on skin mobility. It works well for uncircumcised men (the foreskin is the lubricant, in a sense) and for circumcised men with sufficient loose shaft skin. Advantages: no mess, no preparation, distinct “gliding” sensation. Disadvantages: higher friction, more skin stress, tends to encourage tighter grip, and eliminates a whole category of glans/frenulum techniques that only work with glide.

Lubricated masturbation allows the hand to slide over the skin. Advantages: dramatically lower skin stress; opens up glans, frenulum, and twist techniques; more similar to partnered sex sensation (which is relevant for conditioning); allows lighter pressure to produce more sensation. Disadvantages: mess, preparation, cost, and some men initially find the sensation “too slippery” or less intense — this usually passes within a few sessions as sensitivity recalibrates.

Recommendation: every man should be competent in both. If you’ve only ever done one, spend two weeks doing the other. Chapter 7 covers lubricant selection in detail.

6.7 Circumcised vs. uncircumcised technique notes#

Uncircumcised:

  • The foreskin’s gliding motion over the glans is the primary mechanism. Strokes that move the skin — rather than sliding over it — are usually most effective.
  • The glans is typically more sensitive (protected tissue). Direct glans stimulation may be intense; approach gradually.
  • Lube is optional but adds the sliding category of sensations. Lube under the foreskin, directly on the glans, then stroking with the foreskin partially covering it, is a distinct and popular technique.
  • Foreskin can be held forward over the glans and the hand can stroke the outer foreskin, stimulating the glans through it — a gentler, diffuse sensation.
  • The ridged band (inside the tip of the foreskin) is a specific zone; light fingertip pressure there while the foreskin is partially retracted is worth exploring.
  • Cleanliness under the foreskin before and after matters more (Chapter 5).

Circumcised:

  • Less mobile skin means the hand slides more than it glides. Lube becomes much more important — for both sensation and skin protection.
  • The glans is typically less sensitive (keratinized from exposure), which means direct glans techniques are often more tolerable and more useful.
  • The scar line and remaining frenulum tissue are usually the most sensitive zones; find yours precisely.
  • With lube, the full range of techniques is available. Without it, the repertoire is narrower and skin stress is higher.

6.8 Positions#

Position changes blood flow, muscle engagement, and which hands and zones are accessible.

  • Supine (on back): the classic. Relaxed; both hands free; easy access to everything; good for long sessions. Pillows under the knees relax the pelvic floor.
  • Sitting: convenient, often at a screen. Tends to hunch the body and tense the pelvic floor; less full-body. Lean back and support the spine if you use it.
  • Standing: increases pelvic floor and leg engagement; erections are often firmer (gravity, muscle tone); some men find orgasm more intense standing. Knees soft, not locked.
  • Kneeling / all fours: excellent access to the perineum and anus with one hand; engages the core; a common position for prostate exploration.
  • Prone (face down): see cautions in Chapter 5. Fine in moderation on a soft surface; limits hand technique.
  • Side-lying: relaxed; one hand for the penis, the other easily reaches the perineum or anus; also good for prostate toys.
  • Squatting / on a chair edge / legs elevated: opens the pelvis; changes perineal and anal access; can intensify pelvic floor sensations.

Try changing position mid-session. Moving from sitting to standing when close to orgasm, or from supine to kneeling to reach the perineum, changes the character of the experience.

6.9 Building a session: structure#

A basic template that embodies the principles above:

  1. Warm-up (2–5 min, arousal 1→4). Not touching the penis yet, or barely. Hands on thighs, abdomen, chest, scrotum. Breathing slow. Let arousal come to you. If using visual/fantasy material, start here.
  2. Build (5–15 min, 4→7). Slow strokes, light-to-moderate pressure, lubricated. Vary grip and stroke every minute or two. Notice which zones respond today.
  3. Plateau (5–30+ min, 7→8.5, hovering). This is the heart of the session and the subject of Chapters 9 and 11. Sustain high arousal without tipping over. Change technique. Add secondary zones. Approach 9, back off to 7, approach again.
  4. Approach (1–3 min, 8.5→9.5). Decide to finish. Choose the technique and speed. Slow and light produces one kind of orgasm; fast and firm another. Neither is “correct.”
  5. Orgasm (5–20 sec, 10). Continue stimulation through the contractions or stop at the first pulse — different men prefer different things. Pelvic floor engagement (Chapter 10) intensifies contractions.
  6. Afterglow (2–10 min). Don’t jump up. Breathe. Notice the body. This is where the prolactin/oxytocin relaxation lives, and it’s better with attention.

Total: 20–60 minutes for a full session. This is not the only kind of session — a 4-minute quickie has its place — but the full version is where the skills develop.

6.10 The most common technique mistakes#

  1. One technique, always. Habituation flattens sensation. Rotate.
  2. Too fast, too soon. Skipping the build robs the orgasm of intensity.
  3. Too tight. Conditioning, skin stress, reduced nuance.
  4. Dry (for circumcised men especially). Unnecessary friction; narrows the repertoire.
  5. Glans neglect or glans overload. Either ignoring the most sensitive tissue or grinding it into numbness.
  6. Ignoring everything but the penis. Scrotum, perineum, nipples, thighs — untouched.
  7. Fixed attention on a screen. Sensation is amplified by attention to sensation, not to pixels (Chapter 8).
  8. Tense body. Clenched jaw, held breath, hunched shoulders — all sympathetic, all working against you.
  9. Racing past 9. Not knowing where you are on the arousal scale until it’s over.
  10. Getting up immediately afterward. Skipping the afterglow.

6.11 A technique inventory to work through#

Over the next several sessions, try each of these once, slowly, with lube, for at least 2 minutes, and note how each feels (see the journaling templates in Chapter 21):

  1. Full grip, overhand, full-length, moderate pressure
  2. Full grip, underhand
  3. Ring grip over the corona only
  4. Two-finger frenulum circles
  5. Palm on glans, circular
  6. Palm flat, penis against abdomen, up-down
  7. Base-only strokes, no glans contact
  8. Squeeze-release pulsing, no friction
  9. Slow twist, full length
  10. Static hold with pelvic floor pulses
  11. Alternating hands, single direction
  12. Non-dominant hand only, full session
  13. Tapping the glans and frenulum
  14. Stroking with one hand while cupping/tugging the scrotum with the other
  15. Stroking with one hand while pressing the perineum with the other
  16. Standing
  17. Kneeling
  18. Glacial speed (one stroke per 10 seconds) for 5 minutes
  19. Barely-touching pressure for 5 minutes
  20. Uncircumcised: foreskin forward, stroking through it; lube under the foreskin. Circumcised: scar-line focus with fingertips.

By the end of this inventory you will know more about your own body than most men learn in a lifetime, and you will have a repertoire instead of a habit.

Key takeaways#

  • Technique is a set of variables — grip, pressure, speed, stroke length, motion, hand, position, lube, zone, rhythm — not a single fixed act.
  • Slower and lighter than your habit is almost always more pleasurable and always safer. Speed and pressure are for finishing, not for the whole session.
  • Build competence in both dry and lubricated technique; lube opens an entire category of sensation and protects skin.
  • Circumcised men rely on sliding and benefit greatly from lube; uncircumcised men have built-in gliding and a more sensitive glans.
  • A full session has a structure: warm-up → build → plateau → approach → orgasm → afterglow. Plateau is where the skill and pleasure live.
  • Work through the 20-item inventory to replace habit with repertoire.
