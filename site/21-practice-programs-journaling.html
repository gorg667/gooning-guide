Chapter 21 — Practice Programs & Journaling#

Everything in this guide becomes real through practice. This chapter converts the material into structured programs — a 4-week foundation, an 8-week intermediate, and an ongoing advanced practice — plus the journaling templates that make progress visible. Pick the one that matches where you are. Adapt freely; the structure is a scaffold, not a rule.

General principles for all programs:

  • Frequency: 3–4 sessions per week is enough for skill development. More is fine if it’s what you’d do anyway; the programs don’t require it.
  • Not every session is a practice session. Quick, unstructured sessions are allowed and encouraged alongside. Just don’t let them be the only kind.
  • Lube, always, for practice sessions.
  • Journal briefly after each practice session. Sixty seconds. The template is at the end.
  • If something hurts, stop. If something isn’t working after two weeks, change it.
  • Pelvic floor exercises (Chapter 10) run in parallel — 5–10 minutes daily, separate from sessions.

21.1 Foundation program (4 weeks)#

For: men who’ve never deliberately examined their technique; men whose sessions are habitually fast, tight, dry, or screen-locked; anyone starting from the default.

Goals: establish lube; learn the arousal scale; break the single-technique habit; introduce slow, attentive practice; begin pelvic floor awareness.

Week 1 — Slow down, lube up#

  • Session 1 (20 min): Chapter 8 transition ritual (5 min). Lubricated, full grip, at half your habitual speed and pressure. Notice the difference. Finish however you like. Journal.
  • Session 2 (20 min): Same, plus: rate your arousal aloud (or mentally) every minute — “5… 6… 7…” Learn where the numbers are.
  • Session 3 (20 min): Non-dominant hand only, lubricated, slow. Notice how much more attention it requires.
  • Daily: Pelvic floor Phase 1 (Chapter 10.4) — 5 minutes of awareness, contraction and release.

Week 2 — Repertoire#

  • Session 1 (25 min): Work through inventory items 1–7 from Chapter 6.11, two minutes each. Journal which felt best.
  • Session 2 (25 min): Items 8–14.
  • Session 3 (25 min): Items 15–20. Pick your three favorites from the whole inventory.
  • Daily: Pelvic floor Phase 1.

Week 3 — Attention#

  • Session 1 (25 min): Mindful masturbation (Chapter 8.6), eyes closed throughout, no screen, no goal. Orgasm optional.
  • Session 2 (25 min): Screen or fantasy for the first 5 minutes to reach arousal 6; then eyes closed, attention on sensation for the rest.
  • Session 3 (25 min): Your three favorite techniques, rotating every 2 minutes, slow, with one hand on a secondary zone (scrotum or perineum) throughout.
  • Daily: Pelvic floor Phase 2 begins (flicks, holds, reverse Kegels).

Week 4 — First edges#

  • Session 1 (30 min): Build slowly to 8. Stop completely. Breathe. Drop to 6. Rebuild. Two cycles, then finish.
  • Session 2 (30 min): Three cycles, stopping at 8.5. Add long exhale and body relaxation at each stop.
  • Session 3 (30 min): Free session using everything from the month. Finish with a technique you’ve never finished with before.
  • Daily: Pelvic floor Phase 2.

Week 4 review: Compare your Week 1 and Week 4 journal entries. Most men see: more sensation at lower pressure, awareness of arousal level, a wider repertoire, longer sessions without trying, and a stronger orgasm after the edged sessions.

21.2 Intermediate program (8 weeks)#

For: men who’ve completed the foundation or who already use lube, know the arousal scale, and have some variety.

Goals: reliable edging; pelvic floor as a tool; two-zone stimulation as default; mapping all erogenous zones; first toy exploration; screen independence; session design.

Weeks 1–2 — Edging proficiency#

  • 3 sessions/week, 30–40 min. Stop-start edging (Chapter 9.3): stop at 8.5, drop to 6–7, rebuild. Target 4–5 cycles. Tools: long exhale, body relaxation, testicle traction. Journal cycles and closest approach.
  • Pelvic floor Phase 2 daily; begin erect practice twice weekly.
  • One screen-free session per week.

Weeks 3–4 — Pelvic floor integration and mapping#

  • Session A (40 min): the erogenous zone mapping exercise (Chapter 11.3) — every zone, three touch types, rated. Journal fully.
  • Session B (40 min): edging with reverse Kegel at each peak. Notice whether it drops arousal for you.
  • Session C (40 min): edging with pelvic floor pulsing during pauses to hold arousal at 7 instead of letting it fall to 6.
  • Repeat pattern week 4, now stopping at 9 rather than 8.5.
  • Pelvic floor Phase 2, progressing holds.

Weeks 5–6 — Layering and toys#

  • Session A (40 min): edging with two zones throughout — penis plus the highest-rated secondary zone from your mapping. Add a third receptor type (temperature via warmed/cooled lube or a cloth; or stretch via scrotal traction).
  • Session B (40 min): first toy session — a bullet vibrator on the frenulum and perineum (Chapter 13.3), low setting, moving, alongside hand stimulation. Or, if prostate curiosity: external anal touch only (Chapter 12.1 Stage 1).
  • Session C (30 min): quick session, but with one deliberate difference from your habit (standing; non-dominant hand; different finishing technique).
  • Continue one screen-free session/week; consider a full screen-free week in week 6.

Weeks 7–8 — Continuous edging and session design#

  • Session A (45 min): continuous modulated edging (Chapter 9.5) — no full stops; modulate technique to hold 8–9 for a target of 5 minutes, then 10.
  • Session B (45 min): the 45-minute full session template from Chapter 11.9, followed exactly once, then adapted.
  • Session C (varies): your own design. Journal the design and the result.
  • Pelvic floor Phase 3 begins — the exercises are now mostly in sessions.

Week 8 review: You should have: 5+ edge cycles reliably; reverse Kegel working as a brake; a map of your zones; a two-zone default; comfort with at least one toy; at least one full screen-free week completed; and a clear sense of what your best sessions have in common.

21.3 Advanced practice (ongoing)#

For: men who’ve completed the intermediate program or equivalent.

There’s no fixed schedule; there’s a set of practices to rotate through and deepen. A reasonable rhythm: one “deep” session per week (60–90 min), one or two medium sessions (30–45 min), and quick sessions as desired.

Practices#

Whole-body arousal (Chapter 11.8). Long plateau (20+ min at 8–9), full non-pelvic relaxation, deep belly breathing with imagined direction of sensation outward, nipples and perineum involved, movement, sound. Track: tingling in extremities, warmth spreading, trembling — the markers of the nervous system routing arousal widely.

Prostate development (Chapter 12.1). Weekly or more, 30–45 min, with a toy. Stages 2→5. Combine with penile arousal at 8+ until prostate sensation is clearly pleasurable, then reduce penile input. Track: sensation quality (0–5), any waves or peaks, comfort.

Non-ejaculatory orgasm (Chapter 12.3). Emerges from edging depth. In each deep session, hold at 9 (not 9.5) with total relaxation and breath; at the first involuntary pulses, stop all stimulation and reverse Kegel; observe. Track: peaks without ejaculation, arousal drop afterward, refractory experience. Months, not weeks.

Orgasm engineering (Chapter 11.7). Vary one variable per session: pelvic floor flexed vs. released at orgasm; stimulation continued vs. stopped at first contraction; perineal pressure timed to contractions; slow vs. fast approach; standing vs. lying; exhale with sound vs. silent. Rate each. Build your personal optimal.

Sensory expansion. Rotate through receptor types deliberately: a “temperature session,” a “vibration session,” a “texture session,” a “caress-speed session.” Keep the nervous system surprised.

Range maintenance. Once a month: a deliberately minimal session — hands, no lube, no toys, no screen, ten minutes — to confirm the baseline still works. Once a month: a screen-free week. Once a quarter: two to four days of abstinence followed by a planned deep session (Chapter 11.2).

Pelvic floor maintenance. 5–10 min, 4–5×/week. Always including release work.

Periodic re-mapping. Every 3 months, repeat the erogenous zone mapping. Sensitivity changes with practice — especially nipples and prostate.

Periodic honest check (Chapter 15). Can I skip it easily? Do I enjoy it? Is my life better for it?

21.4 Problem-specific programs#

Cross-references for men working on a specific issue:

  • Premature ejaculation: Chapter 9.8’s 8-week program + Chapter 10’s 12-week pelvic floor program + Chapter 16.1. Emphasis on release, breath, slowness.
  • Delayed ejaculation / idiosyncratic technique: Chapter 16.2’s retraining (suspend the method, substitute partner-like stimulation, reduce frequency, align fantasy) — 4–8 weeks — + Chapter 11.3 mapping for unused inputs + Chapter 8 for spectatoring.
  • Death grip: Chapter 16.3’s 4-week recalibration.
  • Screen dependence / conditioning: Chapter 14.4 — front-load then eyes closed; no browsing; 2–6 week porn break with fantasy-based sessions; audio.
  • Compulsive pattern: Chapter 15.5 — reframe, change configuration (no screens, time limits, not in bed at night, planned sessions), address what’s underneath, structured break, professional help if needed.
  • Hypertonic pelvic floor / pelvic pain: Chapter 10.5 — release emphasis, stretching, breathing, self-massage, physiotherapy.
  • Erectile difficulty (psychogenic): Chapter 16.4 — no-goal sessions with erection unmonitored; mindfulness; cardiovascular basics; pelvic floor; screen break if conditioned; PDE5i to break the cycle if needed.

21.6 A note on progress#

Skill in solo sex follows the same curve as any skill: fast early gains (weeks 1–4 typically produce the most dramatic change), a plateau, then slower deepening over months and years. The plateau is not failure; it’s the stage at which the fundamentals have become automatic and the subtle work begins.

Some things — prostate pleasure, non-ejaculatory orgasm, whole-body arousal — may take many months or may not develop for you. That’s individual variation, not inadequacy. The intermediate states are pleasurable in themselves.

The single most reliable predictor of progress is paying attention — during sessions, and in the journal afterward. Men who track improve. Men who don’t, drift back to habit.

Key takeaways#

  • Foundation (4 weeks): lube, slow down, arousal scale, 20-item repertoire, mindful attention, first edges, pelvic floor awareness.
  • Intermediate (8 weeks): reliable 5+ cycle edging, reverse Kegel as brake, erogenous zone mapping, two-zone default, layered receptor types, first toys, screen-free week, continuous edging, session design.
  • Advanced (ongoing): whole-body arousal, prostate development, non-ejaculatory orgasm, orgasm engineering, sensory rotation, range maintenance, re-mapping, honest checks.
  • Problem-specific programs cross-reference Chapters 9, 10, 14, 15, 16.
  • Journal — 60 seconds per session, a map every quarter, a 5-minute monthly review. Tracking is the strongest predictor of progress.
  • Progress curve: fast early gains, plateau, slow deepening. Individual variation is real; intermediate states are the point, not a consolation.
