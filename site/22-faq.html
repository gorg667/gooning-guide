Chapter 22 — FAQ#

Direct answers to the questions men actually ask. Each points to the chapter with the full discussion.

Frequency and normalcy#

Is it normal to masturbate every day? Yes. Daily masturbation is common, especially under 40, and is not associated with any harm in the absence of compulsivity or physical symptoms. (Ch. 4, 15)

Is it normal to masturbate several times a day? Common in younger men; not harmful by itself. Watch for skin irritation and ask the Chapter 15 questions (control, interference, enjoyment). (Ch. 15)

Is it normal to rarely or never masturbate? Yes. Some men have low solo desire; some have partnered sex that meets their needs; some choose not to. Not masturbating is only a concern if it’s driven by shame or anxiety that’s causing other problems. (Ch. 15.6)

How often do most men masturbate? Enormous range. Roughly: most men under 40 a few times a week; about a third of men “a few times a week or more”; frequency declines with age but with huge overlap. (Ch. 1, 4)

I’m in a relationship. Is it normal to still masturbate? Yes — it’s most common among partnered men aged 25–39. It’s not a sign of dissatisfaction. Secrecy causes more problems than the behavior. (Ch. 15.7, 17.6)

Am I addicted? Frequency doesn’t answer this. Take the self-assessment in Chapter 15.4. The clinical question is: loss of control, real consequences, continued despite them, for 6+ months. Moral guilt alone doesn’t qualify. (Ch. 15)

Health#

Does masturbation lower testosterone? No. Baseline testosterone is unaffected. Brief fluctuations after orgasm or abstinence are small and clinically meaningless. (Ch. 4.3.1)

Does abstaining raise testosterone? Not meaningfully. The most-cited study (a 7-day spike) is small, has been flagged as retracted, and shows a return to baseline afterward. (Ch. 4.3.1, 4.5)

Does it cause hair loss? No. Hair loss is genetic and androgen-mediated; masturbation doesn’t alter androgens. (Ch. 4.3.3)

Does it cause erectile dysfunction? Ordinary masturbation doesn’t. Very specific patterns — compulsive use, extreme grip, exclusive reliance on one stimulus — may contribute to situational difficulties in some men, and these are reversible. (Ch. 4.3.2, 14, 16.4)

Does it cause premature ejaculation? Rushed, fast, tense masturbation can train a fast reflex. Slow, attentive masturbation with stop-start practice treats PE. The technique matters, not the act. (Ch. 9.8, 16.1)

Does it affect the prostate? The largest study found men ejaculating ≥21 times/month had ~20% lower prostate cancer diagnosis risk than men at 4–7/month. Correlational, not proven. Certainly not harmful. (Ch. 4.2.1)

Does it affect fertility or sperm quality? Frequent ejaculation (every 1–3 days) is associated with better sperm DNA quality. Long abstinence increases volume but reduces motility. (Ch. 4.2.8)

Does it “waste” energy or semen? No. Semen is continuously produced; there’s no finite reservoir. The subjective energy some men feel during abstinence is real but isn’t from “retention.” (Ch. 4.3.4, 19)

Is it good for anything? Stress relief, sleep, possibly pain relief, pelvic floor and erectile maintenance, self-knowledge, sexual skill, and pleasure. (Ch. 4.2)

Can I hurt myself? Friction burns, frenulum tears, and bruising are common and preventable with lube and moderate grip. Penile fracture (from bending an erect penis forcefully) and urethral injury (from inserting objects) are rare and serious. (Ch. 5)

Why do I feel sad or empty afterward? Post-coital dysphoria — reported by ~40% of men at some point. Usually brief; hormonal shift and/or surfaced emotion. If it’s shame-driven, Chapter 4 and 19 help. If persistent and distressing, a therapist. (Ch. 16.9)

Why do I feel sick for days after orgasm? Possibly post-orgasmic illness syndrome (POIS) — rare, real, not psychological. See a urologist. (Ch. 16.8)

Is blood in semen serious? Almost always benign, especially under 40 — a small vessel from vigorous activity. Recurrent, or over 40, or with other symptoms — see a doctor. (Ch. 16.6)

My testicles ache after a long session without orgasm. Is that dangerous? “Blue balls” (vasocongestion) is real, benign, and resolves with ejaculation or time. Sudden severe testicular pain is different — emergency. (Ch. 16.5)

Technique#

I’ve always done it one way. Does it matter? Yes. A single technique habituates (less pleasure over time) and can condition a narrow threshold (harder to orgasm any other way). Variety is both more pleasurable and protective. (Ch. 6)

Should I use lube? Almost always yes for circumcised men; often for uncircumcised. It protects skin, adds sensation detail, and opens techniques impossible dry. (Ch. 6.6, 7)

What lube? Silicone for hand sessions (lasts, few ingredients). Water-based for toys (check osmolality for anal). Coconut oil or jojoba if you want natural and don’t use latex or TPE toys. Never numbing lubes. (Ch. 7)

How hard should I grip? Lighter than you think. If you leave red marks or need a tight grip to feel anything, that’s conditioning. Recalibrate over 4 weeks. (Ch. 6.5, 16.3)

How fast? Slow for most of the session. Speed is for finishing, if at all. Most of the pleasure is in the slow build. (Ch. 6.4)

What’s the most sensitive part? For most men, the frenulum, then the corona and glans. Direct, lubricated fingertip attention to the frenulum is the highest-yield single technique. (Ch. 2.2, 6.2.3)

I’m uncircumcised — anything different? Your foreskin provides gliding; lube is optional but adds a category. Glans is usually more sensitive — approach gradually. Lube under the foreskin with the foreskin gliding over it is a distinct technique. Return the foreskin after washing. (Ch. 6.7, 5.1)

I’m circumcised — anything different? Lube is more important (sliding, not gliding). Glans is usually less sensitive, so direct glans techniques are more usable. Find your scar line — it’s often the most sensitive zone. (Ch. 6.7)

What’s the best position? Supine for relaxation and long sessions; standing for firmer erections and intense orgasm; kneeling/side-lying for perineal and anal access. Vary. (Ch. 6.8)

How do I last longer? Learn the arousal scale; stop-start at 8.5; long exhales; relax the body; reverse Kegel at the edge; slow down the whole session; pelvic floor training. 8–12 weeks. (Ch. 9, 10, 16.1)

How do I finish faster / at all? If you struggle to orgasm: identify and suspend any idiosyncratic method (prone, death grip, specific fantasy), substitute partner-like stimulation, reduce frequency for a few weeks, add inputs (vibration, prostate, zones), stop watching yourself. (Ch. 16.2)

What is edging? Approaching orgasm, backing off, repeating. Extends sessions and intensifies the eventual orgasm. Also the behavioral treatment for PE. (Ch. 9)

How do I make orgasms more intense? Longer build-up (20–60 min), 5–10 edge cycles, two zones at once, pelvic floor strength, perineal pressure timed to contractions, long exhale with sound, full attention, no screen at the peak. (Ch. 11.7)

Why does my erection go soft during long sessions? Normal. Erections fluctuate with stimulation level and attention. It returns. Pelvic floor contractions can restore it. Not a problem. (Ch. 3.2, 9.10)

Can I orgasm without an erection? Yes. Full erection isn’t required for orgasm or ejaculation. (Ch. 3.9)

Pleasure#

What am I missing? Most men: the build-up (rushing to orgasm), secondary zones (scrotum, perineum, nipples), attention (on a screen instead of the body), variety (one technique), the afterglow (getting up immediately). (Ch. 11)

What secondary zones are worth trying? Scrotum (cupping, tugging), perineum (firm pressure), nipples (develops with practice), inner thighs, external anal touch, and the prostate. Map them once. (Ch. 11.3)

Does nipple stimulation do anything for men? About half of men report it increases arousal; sensitivity often develops with practice over weeks. (Ch. 2.7, 11.3)

Should I try prostate stimulation? If you’re curious, yes — it’s the largest addition available, orientation-irrelevant, safe with basic precautions, and often intensifies penile orgasm even before it does anything on its own. Weeks to months. (Ch. 12.1)

Does prostate play mean anything about my orientation? No. The prostate is innervated identically in all men. (Ch. 12.1)

What is a “full-body orgasm”? Orgasmic sensation spreading beyond the pelvis — built from deep relaxation, breath, attention widening, multiple zones, movement, and sustained plateau. Develops over weeks. (Ch. 11.8)

Can men have multiple orgasms? Yes, via non-ejaculatory orgasm (prostate route, edging-derived dry peaks, or the clench) — because the refractory period tracks ejaculation, not orgasm. Months of practice; not everyone gets there. (Ch. 12.3)

Do I need toys? No. They add sensations hands can’t produce (vibration, enveloping pressure, internal stimulation). A bullet vibrator is the best first purchase. (Ch. 13)

Why does a vibrator feel so different? It recruits Pacinian receptors (deep vibration) that hands can’t stimulate efficiently. (Ch. 11.3, 13.3)

Is it better with or without porn? For arousal (“wanting”), porn is efficient. For sensation (“feeling”), it competes for attention. Most men find the hybrid — material to get to 6–7, then eyes closed — gives stronger orgasms than continuous watching. (Ch. 8.10, 14.4)

How long should a session be? Any length. Skill develops in 20–60 minute sessions; pleasure peaks for most men at 30–60 minutes of plateau; beyond ~90 minutes returns diminish. Quick sessions have their place. (Ch. 6.9, 9.10)

Is morning or evening better? Morning often produces firmer erections and more sensation (testosterone peak, rested). Evening is more convenient. Late-night exhausted sessions are the worst version. (Ch. 11.4)

How long should I wait between sessions for the best orgasm? 2–4 days for most men. Beyond a week, some men find sensitivity paradoxically drops. (Ch. 11.4)

Pornography and fantasy#

Is watching porn bad for me? For most men, no measurable harm. For a minority, compulsive use causes real problems. Porn is fiction and a poor educator. Keep the range wide; watch for the pattern, not the number. (Ch. 14)

Does porn cause ED? Not porn per se. Problematic/compulsive use is associated with ED; conditioning and anxiety are the likely mechanisms; it’s reversible. (Ch. 14.3)

I can only get hard to porn. What do I do? A 2–6 week break from porn (not necessarily from masturbation), with fantasy-based, attentive sessions. Arousal to non-screen stimuli typically returns in 2–4 weeks. (Ch. 14.4)

Is my fantasy content a problem? If it’s legal and stays in your head, no. Fantasy isn’t a wish or a diagnosis; nearly everyone has fantasies they’d never want to enact. (Ch. 14.1)

What is “gooning”? Prolonged edging with continuous porn, seeking a trance-like state. As an occasional intensity setting — a legitimate choice. As a nightly, escalating, identity-forming pattern that displaces sleep and life — a description of compulsive behavior. (Ch. 15.3)

Should I do NoFap / a reboot? Abstinence has no physiological benefits. The psychological benefits some men report are real and likely come from breaking a compulsive pattern. A structured break from porn is what most men who benefit actually needed; masturbation itself isn’t the problem. Avoid shame-based framing. (Ch. 4.5, 14.4)

Safety#

Is anal play safe? Yes with: lots of low-osmolality lube, flared-base toys only, slow entry at high arousal, no numbing agents, washing before anything touches the penis. (Ch. 5.7, 12.1)

Can I use a household object? No. Not in the anus (retrieval surgery), not in the urethra (strictures), not as a ring (emergency removal), not a vacuum cleaner. Only things designed for the purpose. (Ch. 5.3)

How long can I wear a cock ring? 20–30 minutes maximum. Remove at any pain, numbness, cold, or color change. Never sleep in one. Never an unsized rigid ring. (Ch. 13.4)

Is a lump on my penis or testicle serious? Pearly penile papules, Fordyce spots, and sebaceous cysts are normal. A hard lump in a testicle needs a doctor within days. A plaque in the shaft with new curvature — urologist. (Ch. 16.15)

When should I see a doctor? Emergencies: erection >4 hours, popping sound + swelling, sudden severe testicular pain, foreskin stuck retracted, stuck object, first thunderclap headache at orgasm. Soon: recurrent blood in semen, testicular lump, new curvature, sores/warts, persistent numbness. (Ch. 5.9, 16.16)

Is breath play / choking during masturbation ever safe alone? No. It kills. This is the only flat prohibition in the guide. (Ch. 12.5)

Life and relationships#

Should I tell my partner I masturbate? Yes, in general terms. Secrecy is corrosive; detail is optional. (Ch. 15.7, 17.6)

My partner is upset that I masturbate. What now? Find out why — insecurity, moral belief, deception, specific concern about porn or frequency. Each has a different response. Total prohibition is an unusual demand; porn negotiation is common. (Ch. 17.6)

I’m over 50 and things are changing. Is that normal? Yes. More stimulation needed, softer erections, longer refractory, less volume. Adapt: more time, more zones, vibration, prostate, pelvic floor, PDE5i if wanted. Erection isn’t required for orgasm. (Ch. 17.3)

I’m on antidepressants and can’t finish. Options? Talk to the prescriber: dose, switching (bupropion, mirtazapine), adjuncts, timing. Add vibration and inputs. Don’t stop the medication on your own. (Ch. 16.2, 18.6)

Can I masturbate the night before a competition/exam? Yes. The “sex before competition” myth is unsupported. Immediately before (within an hour), individual. (Ch. 17.8)

I have no privacy. What can I do? Shower sessions (silicone lube), timing, headphones, locked storage, a 15-minute quiet mindful session is achievable. (Ch. 17.8)

Miscellaneous#

Why is my semen yellow / clumpy / watery? Yellow after abstinence: normal. Clumpy/gel then liquefying: normal. Watery with frequent ejaculation: normal. Brown/red: blood (usually benign). Green/grey with pain: infection. (Ch. 16.14)

Why does the first ejaculation after a long gap have more volume? Seminal vesicles and prostate fill over 3–5 days, then plateau. (Ch. 3.4)

Why can’t I get hard again right after? The refractory period — likely prolactin-driven, tied to ejaculation. Minutes in youth, hours later. Non-ejaculatory orgasms have little or none. (Ch. 3.7)

Is my penis normal? Almost certainly. Average erect length ~13 cm; curvature <30° is normal; size varies more flaccid than erect; nerve density, not length, determines what you feel. (Ch. 2.8)

What’s the single most useful thing in this guide? Slow down, use lube, and put your attention on the sensation instead of the screen or the finish line. Everything else builds on that. (Ch. 6, 8, 11)
