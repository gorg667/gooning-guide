## An evidence-informed handbook on masturbation mastery, pleasure, health, and self-knowledge

---

## How to use this guide

This is a long document. It is designed to be read either cover-to-cover or as a reference. Every chapter is self-contained, cross-references other chapters where useful, and ends with a **Key takeaways** box.

**If you are a beginner** — read Chapters 1–8 in order, then jump to Chapter 20 (Practice Programs) and start the 4-week foundation program.

**If you are experienced and want more pleasure** — go to Chapters 9–12 (edging, pelvic floor, pleasure maximization, advanced sensations, toys), then Chapter 20's intermediate and advanced programs.

**If something is wrong** — pain, numbness, difficulty finishing, finishing too fast, erection problems, feeling out of control — go to Chapters 14 and 15 first, then come back.

**If you just want facts** — Chapter 4 (what the science says), Chapter 13 (pornography), Chapter 21 (FAQ), and Chapter 23 (references).

### Conventions used

- **Evidence ratings.** When we make a claim about health or physiology we tag it:
  - 🟢 **Strong** — multiple good studies or broad clinical consensus.
  - 🟡 **Moderate** — some studies, mechanistically plausible, but limited or mixed.
  - 🟠 **Weak / anecdotal** — case reports, community lore, or plausible theory without direct testing.
  - 🔴 **Contradicted** — popular belief that the evidence goes against.
- **"Practice" boxes** contain concrete exercises.
- **"Caution" boxes** flag risk.
- Numbers in square brackets like [Rider 2016] refer to the reference list in Chapter 23.

### A note on language

We use "men" and "male" throughout to refer to people with penises, prostates, and testes. Much of the content applies regardless of gender identity. Where we discuss trans men or intersex readers, we do so specifically.

### Philosophy of this guide

Three ideas run through every chapter:

1. **Masturbation is a skill, not just a reflex.** Like any physical practice — cooking, running, playing an instrument — it can be done thoughtlessly or with attention, and attention makes it better in every measurable way: more pleasure, better control, fewer problems, better partnered sex.
2. **Knowledge removes shame.** Almost every anxiety men carry about solo sex — "is it too much?", "am I damaging myself?", "is this normal?" — dissolves under accurate information. We give you the information.
3. **Pleasure and health are allies, not enemies.** The techniques that produce the most intense, satisfying experiences are, almost without exception, the same ones that protect your sexual function long-term: variety, moderate pressure, lubrication, pelvic floor awareness, mindfulness, and not treating orgasm as the only goal.
