# Chapter 7 — Lubricants: The Deep Dive

Lubricant is the most consequential product choice in solo sex. The right one transforms sensation, protects skin, and opens techniques that are impossible dry. The wrong one irritates, degrades toys, or — in the case of anal use — damages tissue. This chapter covers the chemistry, the categories, what to avoid, and how to choose.

## 7.1 Why lubricate at all

- **Sensation.** Friction masks fine-touch detail. A lubricated hand transmits texture, temperature, and pressure variation in a way a dry hand cannot. Most men who switch from dry to lubricated report initially "less intense, more detailed," then, after adaptation, "much better."
- **Skin protection.** Friction damage — micro-tears, redness, calluses — is the most common masturbation injury (Chapter 5). Lube nearly eliminates it.
- **Technique range.** Glans polishing, frenulum circles, twists, palm techniques — all require glide.
- **Conditioning.** Partnered sex is wet. Training exclusively with dry, high-friction stimulation can make the wetter, softer sensation of a partner feel insufficient [Perelman 2016]. Lubricated solo practice keeps the sensory expectation realistic.
- **Toys.** Sleeves and prostate toys require lube; dry use is uncomfortable and damages both you and the toy.
- **Anal.** Non-negotiable. The rectum produces no lubrication.

## 7.2 The four families

### 7.2.1 Water-based

**Composition:** water + a thickener (cellulose derivatives, carbomers, xanthan) + humectants (glycerin, propylene glycol, or newer alternatives) + preservatives (parabens, phenoxyethanol, potassium sorbate) + pH adjusters.

**Pros:** compatible with everything (latex, polyisoprene, silicone toys, TPE sleeves); easy cleanup with water; non-staining; widely available; huge variety.

**Cons:** dries out as water evaporates/absorbs — needs reapplication or a spritz of water to reactivate (this is the most common complaint); can get sticky or tacky as it dries, especially glycerin-heavy formulas; many formulas are hyperosmolar (see 7.4) and irritating for some users; preservatives are the most common allergens.

**Best for:** general use, toy use, anyone who wants easy cleanup, anal use *if* the osmolality is reasonable.

**Sub-types:**
- *Glycerin-based*: cheap, slick, slightly sweet, but high osmolality and can feed yeast in prone individuals. Common in drugstore brands.
- *Glycerin-free / "natural"*: often use aloe, carrageenan, or hydroxyethylcellulose. Usually lower osmolality, less sticky, less irritating. Dry out faster.
- *Thick / gel*: stays in place; good for anal.
- *Thin / liquid*: spreads easily; good for full-hand techniques.
- *Cream-textured hybrids*: see 7.2.4.

### 7.2.2 Silicone-based

**Composition:** dimethicone, dimethiconol, cyclomethicone, and related polymers. No water.

**Pros:** extremely long-lasting — does not dry out or absorb; a small amount goes a long way; silky rather than slick; waterproof (works in the shower); hypoallergenic for most people; iso-osmotic-irrelevant (no water to draw); doesn't feed yeast.

**Cons:** should not be used with silicone toys (the lube's solvents can degrade the toy's surface, making it sticky and porous — the effect is real, especially with lower-grade silicone toys, though high-quality platinum silicone is more resistant; the safe rule is "don't"); requires soap to remove; can stain fabrics; more expensive per bottle (but far less used per session); some people find the texture "too slippery" or unnatural.

**Best for:** long sessions, hand-only or with non-silicone toys (glass, steel, ABS, TPE sleeves), shower use, anal play (excellent staying power), men whose skin reacts to water-based preservatives.

**Note on TPE sleeves:** most sleeve manufacturers recommend water-based only. Silicone lube on TPE is generally fine for the material itself, but check the manufacturer's guidance — some TPE formulations do degrade.

### 7.2.3 Oil-based

**Composition:** plant oils (coconut, almond, olive, sunflower), mineral oil, petroleum jelly, or commercial oil-based blends.

**Pros:** very long-lasting; excellent for skin (moisturizing); cheap and often already in the house; natural options available; good for massage that transitions into genital touch.

**Cons:** **destroys latex condoms** (irrelevant for solo, critical for partnered); can degrade some toy materials; hard to clean; stains fabrics; coconut oil is comedogenic for some (acne on nearby skin); mineral oil/petroleum jelly should not be used anally by anyone who might later have receptive anal sex with a condom, and some data associates petroleum-based products with altered vaginal flora (not relevant to solo male use, but a reason it's not used as a general recommendation); rancidity — plant oils oxidize, so use fresh oil and small quantities.

**Best for:** hand-only sessions where cleanup is manageable, men who want a skin-nourishing option, long slow sessions, "massage-first" approaches.

**Common household oils ranked for solo use:**
- *Coconut oil (fractionated or virgin)*: excellent glide, antimicrobial properties, pleasant; can clog pores in the groin for some men.
- *Sweet almond oil*: light, absorbs slowly, minimal odor. Nut allergy caution.
- *Olive oil*: works; heavier; distinct smell.
- *Jojoba*: technically a wax ester; close to skin's own sebum; very well tolerated.
- *Mineral oil / baby oil*: works; petroleum-derived; some prefer to avoid; check that "baby oil" isn't fragranced.
- *Petroleum jelly*: works but thick; hard to remove; best as a barrier for healing skin rather than a primary lube.
- **Avoid:** butter, margarine, cooking oils that go rancid quickly, anything with added fragrance, lotions with alcohol or fragrance.

### 7.2.4 Hybrids and specialty

**Hybrid (water + silicone)**: creamy texture, longer-lasting than pure water-based, easier cleanup than pure silicone. Small silicone percentage means usually (not always) toy-safe — check the label. Popular "middle ground."

**Cream / lotion-type lubes**: emulsions, often oil-in-water. Thick, opaque, very good for hand-only sessions; not for toys or condoms unless specified.

**Warming / cooling / tingling**: contain menthol, capsaicin derivatives, niacin, or peppermint. Novelty sensation. Irritating for many; keep off the urethral opening; never use anally.

**Flavored**: for oral use; typically high in sugar/glycerin; irrelevant for solo.

**"Natural"/organic**: marketing term. Look at the actual ingredients and osmolality.

**Anal-specific**: usually thicker gels; sometimes contain numbing agents (benzocaine, lidocaine) — **avoid numbing lubes**. Pain is information. Numbing allows injury you won't feel until later.

**Masturbation-specific creams** (e.g., products marketed as "stroke" lotions): usually hybrid or oil-based emulsions optimized for hand use. Often excellent for the purpose.

## 7.4 Osmolality and pH: the science most guides skip

**Osmolality** is the concentration of dissolved particles in a solution. Body tissues sit at roughly 280–300 mOsm/kg. A lubricant with much higher osmolality (**hyperosmolar**) pulls water out of the epithelial cells it contacts, causing them to shrink, slough, and lose barrier integrity. In laboratory tissue models, hyperosmolar lubricants markedly reduced epithelial barrier function [Ayehunie 2017], and in a human study, a hyperosmolar lubricant caused visible epithelial damage in the distal colon within 60–90 minutes [Fuchs 2007].

The **World Health Organization** (2012 advisory note on lubricants for condom use) recommended that lubricants ideally have osmolality below **380 mOsm/kg**, with an interim maximum of **1,200 mOsm/kg**. Many popular commercial water-based lubricants measure 2,000–6,000+ mOsm/kg — 10 to 20 times body tissue. The culprits are typically high glycerin and propylene glycol content.

**Why this matters for men:**

- **Anal use:** rectal epithelium is a single cell layer and highly vulnerable. Hyperosmolar lube → micro-damage → irritation, and in partnered contexts, increased STI transmission risk. Choose iso-osmotic or near-iso-osmotic lubes for anything anal.
- **Penile use:** the penile shaft skin is keratinized and tough; osmolality matters less. But the glans, inner foreskin, and urethral opening are mucosal-type tissue. Men who experience stinging, redness, or a "dried out" feeling after using lube are often reacting to osmolality, not to an allergen.

**pH.** Rectal pH is around 7; a lube for anal use should be near-neutral (5.5–7). Highly acidic lubes (formulated for vaginal use around pH 4) are not ideal for anal use.

**How to find out.** Osmolality is rarely printed on the label, but several independent testers and some manufacturers publish it. Iso-osmotic brands market this fact. Rules of thumb: glycerin-free and propylene-glycol-free water-based lubes are usually much lower; silicone and oil lubes have no relevant osmolality (no water phase). If a lube stings, it's probably hyperosmolar or contains an irritant — switch.

## 7.6 Choosing: a decision guide

**"I want one bottle for everything hand-related."** → Silicone. Lasts, silky, minimal reapplication, waterproof. Keep it away from silicone toys.

**"I use a TPE stroker."** → Water-based (manufacturer standard). Have a spray bottle of water nearby to reactivate. Or a hybrid if the maker approves.

**"I use a silicone prostate massager."** → Water-based, ideally glycerin-free and low-osmolality. Thick gel formulas stay put.

**"My skin gets irritated by lubes."** → Try silicone first (fewest ingredients, no water-phase preservatives). If not, a glycerin-free, paraben-free, fragrance-free water-based. Patch test on the inner forearm for 24 hours.

**"I want something natural and I don't use toys."** → Fractionated coconut oil or jojoba. Small quantities. Fresh bottles.

**"I want to try anal play."** → Thick water-based with low osmolality, or silicone. Never numbing. Lots of it.

**"I want the most intense glans sensation."** → Thin water-based or silicone; the glide lets the hand move fast and light over the head.

**"I want long, slow sessions."** → Silicone or a good hybrid cream. Both last 30–60 minutes without reapplication.

**"I'm on a budget."** → Silicone lube is the best value per session despite the higher bottle price. Or coconut oil.

## 7.7 Application technique

- **Amount.** Start with a coin-sized dollop for a hand session; more for toys; a lot more for anal. Under-lubing is the common error. You should be able to stroke with the lightest pressure and have the hand glide.
- **Warm it.** Cold lube on the glans is unpleasant and briefly suppresses arousal. Rub it between palms for a few seconds, or keep the bottle in a pocket/under a pillow beforehand.
- **Where.** Glans, frenulum, and shaft. Under the foreskin if uncircumcised (retract, apply to the glans, let the foreskin return, then stroke with the foreskin gliding over a lubricated glans — a highly effective technique).
- **Reapply.** Water-based: when it starts to feel tacky, either add more or add a few drops of water (which reactivates most formulas). Silicone: rarely needed.
- **Layering.** A thin layer of silicone under water-based ("silicone base coat") extends the water-based lube's life. Some men use oil on the skin as a base and water-based on top for toy compatibility (check the toy).
- **Cleanup.** Water-based: rinse. Silicone: soap and water, or a wipe first. Oil: soap; a bit of dish soap cuts oil quickly if needed.

## 7.8 DIY and household substitutes — cautions

Things people reach for, and whether they should:

- **Hand lotion / body lotion**: often contain fragrance, alcohol, and irritants. Some plain, fragrance-free lotions work; most are a bad idea on the glans. Not for anal.
- **Saliva**: works briefly; dries fast; not a real lubricant; fine in a pinch for hand use.
- **Soap / shampoo / conditioner**: no. Alkaline, drying, irritating to the urethra. Conditioner is the least bad (it's essentially a cationic emulsion) but still not recommended.
- **Aloe vera gel**: pure aloe (no alcohol, no lidocaine) works reasonably as a light water-based lube. Check the ingredients — "aloe gel" from a pharmacy often has alcohol.
- **Egg whites, yogurt, honey**: no. Sugar, protein, bacteria.
- **Vaseline**: works as a hand lube; hard to clean; not for anal; not with latex.
- **Cooking spray**: contains propellants and additives; no.
- **Vegetable glycerin (pure)**: extremely hyperosmolar; sticky. No.
- **Cornstarch + water (homemade lube)**: a folk recipe (boil 1 tbsp cornstarch in 1 cup water, cool). Actually works reasonably for hand use, is iso-osmotic-ish, and is cheap. Spoils in 1–2 days; refrigerate; make small batches. Not for anal.

**General rule:** if it's not designed for the mucosa or you wouldn't put it in your mouth, be cautious about putting it on your glans.

## 7.9 Storage and shelf life

- Keep lube out of heat and direct sunlight.
- Water-based: typically 1–3 years sealed; 6–12 months after opening. Discard if it changes color, smell, or texture, or separates.
- Silicone: very stable; years.
- Oils: rancidity is the issue. Use within a few months of opening; smell it — rancid oil smells like old crayons or stale nuts.
- Pump bottles are more hygienic than open-mouth jars (fingers contaminate jars).

### Key takeaways

- Lubricant increases **sensation detail**, protects **skin**, opens **technique range**, and keeps sensory expectations **realistic** for partnered sex. Every man should use it at least some of the time; circumcised men, most of the time.
- **Water-based**: universal compatibility, dries out. **Silicone**: lasts forever, not with silicone toys. **Oil**: skin-friendly, kills latex, messy. **Hybrid**: middle ground.
- **Osmolality matters**, especially anally. Ideal <380 mOsm/kg; avoid >1,200. High glycerin/propylene glycol = high osmolality. Stinging = switch.
- **Avoid**: nonoxynol-9, numbing agents, fragrance, chlorhexidine, polyquaternium-15, alcohol, sugar.
- **Use more than you think.** Warm it. Reapply or add water when tacky.
- Household items: coconut oil, jojoba, pure aloe, and homemade cornstarch lube work for hand use. Soap, lotion with fragrance, and food products don't.
