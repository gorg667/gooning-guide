# Chapter 10 — The Pelvic Floor: Kegels, Reverse Kegels & Beyond

The pelvic floor is the only part of the male sexual system that's both directly involved in erection, ejaculation, and orgasm *and* under voluntary control. Training it — in both directions, contraction and release — is the physical foundation for arousal control, orgasm intensity, ejaculatory force, and the advanced practices in Chapter 12. It's also an evidence-based treatment for premature ejaculation and a contributor to erectile function. This chapter is the training manual.

## 10.1 What the pelvic floor does during sex

Review from Chapter 2, with function emphasized:

| Muscle | Sexual function | Trained how |
|---|---|---|
| **Bulbospongiosus** | Rhythmic contractions expel semen; compresses the bulb to increase glans engorgement; its contractions are much of what you *feel* as orgasm | Kegels (the "push out the last drop of urine" squeeze) |
| **Ischiocavernosus** | Compresses the roots of the penis, increasing rigidity ("flexing" the erection) | Kegels (the "lift the erection" squeeze) |
| **Pubococcygeus / levator ani** | Supports pelvic organs; involved in the sense of "urgency" before ejaculation; its involuntary contraction is the 8.5–9 signal | Kegels and, critically, reverse Kegels |
| **External anal sphincter** | Co-contracts with the rest; anal contractions during orgasm | Same |
| **External urethral sphincter** | Closes during emission; opens during expulsion | Same |

**Erection:** a strong ischiocavernosus/bulbospongiosus system improves rigidity and helps maintain erection by compressing venous outflow. In a randomized trial, 40% of men with ED regained normal function and a further 35% improved after 3 months of pelvic floor exercises plus lifestyle advice [Dorey 2005].

**Ejaculatory control:** in lifelong PE, 12 weeks of pelvic floor rehabilitation improved control in 82.5% of men and increased latency from ~32 seconds to ~146 seconds on average [Pastore 2014]. Longer-term follow-up found roughly half maintained the benefit at several years [Pastore 2018]. The mechanism is thought to be both strength (ability to inhibit the expulsion reflex) and, importantly, *awareness* — men learn to feel what the pelvic floor is doing and to relax it.

**Orgasm intensity and ejaculatory force:** anecdotally near-universal; mechanistically obvious (stronger, better-coordinated muscles → stronger contractions → more forceful expulsion and more intense sensation). Controlled data are sparse, but this is one of the most consistently reported effects of pelvic floor training among men.

## 10.2 Finding the muscles

You need to isolate the pelvic floor from the abdominals, buttocks, and thighs — most beginners squeeze everything.

**Method 1 — Urine stop.** While urinating, stop the stream mid-flow. The muscles you used are the pelvic floor. Don't do this regularly as an exercise — it can interfere with bladder function — just once or twice to identify the sensation.

**Method 2 — The lift.** Sitting or lying, imagine lifting your testicles toward your body without using your hands or abs. Or imagine drawing the perineum upward.

**Method 3 — Anal squeeze.** Squeeze as if stopping gas. The anus tightens and lifts. This recruits the posterior pelvic floor; combine with the lift for the full group.

**Method 4 — Erect check.** With an erection, make it twitch upward. That's the ischiocavernosus/bulbospongiosus — the anterior pelvic floor.

**Method 5 — Palpation.** Place a finger on the perineum (between scrotum and anus). Contract. You should feel the tissue lift and firm. Release; it should drop and soften.

**Checks for correct isolation:**
- Abdomen stays soft (hand on it).
- Buttocks don't clench (you don't rise off the chair).
- Thighs relaxed.
- Breathing normally — not holding breath.
- Nobody watching could tell.

If you can't isolate it, that's common. Start with the anal squeeze — easiest to feel — and build from there. A pelvic floor physiotherapist can teach it in one session.

## 10.3 Two directions: contraction and release

Almost every online Kegel guide teaches only contraction. This is a mistake with consequences.

**The hypertonic pelvic floor.** Many men — especially anxious men, men who sit all day, cyclists, men with a history of pelvic pain, and men who've done years of Kegels without release training — have a pelvic floor that is *chronically tight*, not weak. Symptoms: premature ejaculation, pelvic or perineal pain, pain after ejaculation, urinary hesitancy or frequency, erectile difficulty (a tight floor can compress arteries and nerves), constipation, and difficulty relaxing at high arousal. Chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome is increasingly understood, in many cases, as a pelvic floor tension disorder [Anderson 2005].

For these men, more Kegels make things worse. What they need is **release**.

**The reverse Kegel** is the deliberate relaxation and gentle downward push of the pelvic floor — the sensation of beginning to urinate or gently bearing down. The perineum drops; the anus softens and opens slightly; the base of the penis relaxes.

A healthy pelvic floor can **contract strongly, hold, release completely, and relax at rest.** Both directions. The goal is range and control, not just strength.

## 10.4 The training program

### Phase 1 — Awareness (weeks 1–2)

Daily, 5 minutes, lying down with knees bent.

1. **Breathe** slowly. Notice the pelvic floor moving with breath: it drops slightly on inhale, rises on exhale. Diaphragm and pelvic floor move together.
2. **Contract** gently (30–50% effort) on the exhale. Hold 3 seconds. Focus on *what it feels like*.
3. **Release** on the inhale. Feel it drop. Then let it drop *further* — the reverse Kegel. Hold 3 seconds.
4. Repeat 10 times.
5. Finish with 1 minute of complete relaxation, attention on the perineum feeling soft and heavy.

The goal is the map, not strength.

### Phase 2 — Strength and endurance (weeks 3–8)

Daily, 8–10 minutes. Sitting, standing, or lying; standing is hardest.

**Set A — Quick flicks.** 20 rapid contract-release cycles, ~1/second, full release between. Trains fast-twitch fibers used in expulsion and the quick erection "lift."

**Set B — Holds.** 10 contractions held 5 seconds, 5 seconds full release between. Progress to 10-second holds. Trains the endurance needed to maintain inhibition at high arousal.

**Set C — The elevator.** Contract to 25%, hold 2s; 50%, 2s; 75%, 2s; 100%, 2s; then down 75, 50, 25, release. 2–3 cycles. Trains fine control — partial contraction is what makes the pelvic floor a usable throttle during edging.

**Set D — Reverse Kegels.** 10 deliberate releases, each held 5–10 seconds, normal breath between. Focus on the perineum dropping and anus softening. *Not optional.*

**Set E — Erect practice (2–3×/week).** With an erection: 10 slow contractions feeling the penis lift; 10 flicks; 5 reverse Kegels feeling the base soften. Builds the link between exercise and sexual use.

**Progression:** longer holds, more reps, standing. Don't exceed ~15 minutes/day total.

### Phase 3 — Integration (ongoing)

The pelvic floor becomes a tool used *during* sessions:

- **Arousal pulsing (6–7).** During edging pauses, rhythmic contractions maintain or raise arousal without touch. Many men feel a distinct wave of sensation with each contraction at moderate arousal.
- **Reverse Kegel at the edge (8.5–9).** As involuntary pelvic tension begins, consciously release and bear down gently. For most trained men this drops arousal reliably — the single most useful edging tool.
- **Flex during orgasm.** Strong contractions timed with the involuntary ones intensify them and increase ejaculatory force.
- **Release during orgasm.** Alternatively, deliberately relaxing at orgasm lets the sensation spread and lengthens it for some men. Try both.
- **The clench for non-ejaculatory orgasm.** Chapter 12 — a sustained strong contraction at 9.5 can, with practice, block or reduce ejaculation while allowing orgasm. Not for everyone; has caveats.
- **Erection maintenance.** When the erection softens mid-session, a series of contractions restores rigidity by compressing venous outflow.
- **Post-orgasm.** Gentle contractions expel remaining semen.

## 10.5 Relaxation training for the tight pelvic floor

If you have signs of hypertonicity (PE, pelvic pain, urinary hesitancy, pain after ejaculation, difficulty with reverse Kegels, a perineum that feels hard or tender), emphasize release:

- **Diaphragmatic breathing**, 5 minutes daily, lying down, hand on belly, letting the belly rise and pelvic floor drop on each inhale.
- **Reverse Kegels** as the primary exercise; contractions only at low intensity for awareness.
- **Stretches**: deep squat (heels on a rolled towel if needed), happy baby, child's pose, butterfly, pigeon, hip-flexor lunges. 1–2 minutes each, slow breathing. Tight hips = tight floor.
- **Perineal self-massage.** Lubricated thumb, firm slow pressure on the perineum, find tender spots, hold 30–60 seconds until they soften. A soft massage ball under the perineum while sitting (gently) also works.
- **Heat.** Warm bath; heating pad on perineum/lower abdomen.
- **Internal release.** Advanced; best learned from a pelvic floor physiotherapist: a lubricated finger in the anus pressing on tight levator bands (sideways/backward, not toward the prostate) with sustained pressure. Therapeutic wands exist for this.
- **Reduce contributors**: prolonged sitting (move hourly), cycling without a cut-out saddle, chronic stress, holding urine, habitual clenching (check during the day — is your floor gripping while you work?).

A pelvic floor physiotherapist — yes, they treat men — is the best investment if symptoms are significant.

## 10.6 Overtraining and common errors

- **Too much.** 10–15 minutes daily is plenty. Hundreds of Kegels a day produces hypertonicity, pain, and *worse* control. Sore or tight → cut back, emphasize release.
- **Holding breath.** Creates intra-abdominal pressure pushing *down* on the floor. Always breathe.
- **Bearing down instead of lifting.** Check with a finger: contraction lifts, bearing down bulges.
- **Recruiting abs, glutes, thighs.** Use gentler effort until isolated.
- **Only contracting, never releasing.** Half the training is release.
- **Expecting results in a week.** Neuromuscular changes take 4–6 weeks; strength 8–12. Consistency beats intensity.
- **Urine-stopping as regular practice.** Identification only.

## 10.7 Devices and adjuncts

- **Biofeedback** (probe + display) is used in physiotherapy and is highly effective for men who can't feel the muscles.
- **Electrical stimulation** for very weak floors; rarely needed.
- **Interval timer apps** help adherence.
- **Prostate massagers** (Chapter 13) give feedback and resistance for internal awareness — contracting around the toy is itself an exercise.

## 10.8 The pelvic floor and posture

Anterior pelvic tilt (excess lower-back arch, common in sitters) shortens and tightens the floor. Slouching can weaken it. Neutral pelvis — weight through the sitting bones, level pelvis — lets the floor rest at optimal length. Daytime awareness plus hip mobility work supports everything else here.

## 10.9 A 12-week summary program

| Weeks | Focus | Daily work |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Awareness | 5 min: breath-linked gentle contractions and releases, lying down |
| 3–4 | Foundation | 8 min: 20 flicks, 10×5s holds, 10 reverse Kegels; elevator 2× |
| 5–6 | Build | 10 min: 20 flicks, 10×7s holds, 3 elevators, 10 reverse Kegels; some standing; erect practice 2×/wk |
| 7–8 | Strength | 12 min: 30 flicks, 10×10s holds, 3 elevators, 10 reverse Kegels; standing; erect practice 3×/wk |
| 9–10 | Integration | Continue 10 min; use in sessions: arousal pulsing, reverse Kegel at the edge |
| 11–12 | Refinement | Maintenance 8–10 min; experiment with flex vs. release at orgasm |
| Ongoing | Maintenance | 5–10 min, 4–5×/week; release work always included |

Track: how close to 9 you can hold, erection firmness, orgasm intensity (1–10), ejaculatory force, any pain or tightness.

---

### Key takeaways

- The pelvic floor is the **voluntary control panel** for erection rigidity, ejaculatory timing, and orgasm intensity.
- Evidence: training improves **erectile function** [Dorey 2005] and gives **ejaculatory control** to ~80% of men with lifelong PE [Pastore 2014].
- **Find it**: lift the testicles, stop the stream, squeeze the anus, twitch the erection. Isolate. Breathe.
- **Train both directions.** A **tight floor** is as common as a weak one and causes PE, pain, and ED. With those symptoms, emphasize release.
- **Program**: flicks, holds, elevators, reverse Kegels, erect practice. 10–15 min/day, 12 weeks. Don't overtrain.
- **In sessions**: pulse at 6–7 to build; **reverse Kegel at 9** to back off; flex or release at orgasm to shape it.
- **Pelvic floor physiotherapists treat men.**
