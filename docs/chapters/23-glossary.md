# Chapter 23 — Glossary

**Anejaculation** — Absence of ejaculation despite orgasm or adequate stimulation. May be retrograde (into the bladder) or true (no emission).

**Anhedonic ejaculation** — Ejaculation without the pleasurable sensation of orgasm.

**Anorgasmia** — Inability to reach orgasm despite adequate stimulation and desire.

**Arousal scale** — The 1–10 scale used throughout this guide: 6 cruising, 7 rising, 8 committed, 8.5 approaching, 9 the edge, 9.5 point of no return, 10 orgasm.

**Balanitis** — Inflammation of the glans; usually yeast or bacterial; often triggered by moisture, soap, or diabetes.

**Blue balls (epididymal hypertension)** — Testicular ache from prolonged arousal without ejaculation. Vasocongestion. Benign; resolves.

**BPH (benign prostatic hyperplasia)** — Non-cancerous prostate enlargement common with age; causes urinary symptoms.

**Bulbospongiosus** — Pelvic floor muscle wrapping the bulb of the penis; produces the rhythmic contractions of ejaculation.

**Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands** — Produce pre-ejaculate.

**cGMP** — The second messenger that relaxes penile smooth muscle to allow erection; broken down by PDE5.

**Circumcision** — Surgical removal of the foreskin.

**Clench (Kegel block)** — Sustained maximum pelvic floor contraction at the point of no return to block ejaculation while allowing orgasm.

**Coital cephalalgia** — Headache at or near orgasm. Usually benign; first occurrence warrants evaluation.

**Corona (coronal ridge)** — The flared rim at the base of the glans; nerve-dense.

**Corpora cavernosa** — The paired erectile bodies on the top/sides of the penis.

**Corpus spongiosum** — The erectile body surrounding the urethra on the underside; expands into the glans.

**CPPS (chronic pelvic pain syndrome / chronic non-bacterial prostatitis)** — Persistent pelvic pain often attributed to pelvic floor tension.

**CSBD (Compulsive Sexual Behaviour Disorder)** — ICD-11 code 6C72. Persistent failure to control intense sexual impulses resulting in repetitive behavior, with life impairment, for 6+ months. Excludes distress from moral disapproval alone.

**Death grip** — Lay term for habitual very tight, fast masturbation leading to a learned high stimulation threshold.

**Delayed ejaculation (DE)** — Marked delay in or absence of ejaculation despite adequate stimulation.

**Dhat syndrome** — South Asian culture-bound anxiety syndrome about semen loss.

**Dry orgasm** — Orgasm without ejaculation. See non-ejaculatory orgasm.

**Dysorgasmia** — Painful orgasm/ejaculation.

**Edging** — Repeatedly approaching orgasm and backing off before it occurs.

**Emission** — Phase 1 of ejaculation: semen moves into the prostatic urethra under sympathetic control. The point of no return.

**Epididymis** — Coiled tube on the back of each testis where sperm mature.

**Erectile dysfunction (ED)** — Persistent inability to attain or maintain an erection sufficient for satisfactory sexual activity.

**Expulsion** — Phase 2 of ejaculation: rhythmic pelvic floor contractions propel semen out.

**Fordyce spots** — Small, normal sebaceous glands visible as white/yellow dots on the shaft or scrotum.

**Foreskin (prepuce)** — The retractable double-layered skin covering the glans in uncircumcised men.

**Frenulum** — The band of tissue on the underside connecting glans to shaft/foreskin; usually the most sensitive spot.

**Frenulum breve** — A short frenulum that restricts glans movement or tears during erection.

**Glans** — The head of the penis.

**Gooning** — Prolonged edging with continuous pornography seeking a trance-like state; also the associated subculture.

**Hematospermia** — Blood in semen. Usually benign.

**Hypertonic pelvic floor** — Chronically tight pelvic floor muscles; associated with PE, pelvic pain, ED.

**Idiosyncratic masturbatory style** — Perelman's term for a technique (pressure, speed, position) that a partner can't replicate; a cause of DE.

**IELT (intravaginal ejaculatory latency time)** — Time from penetration to ejaculation; the clinical measure in PE research.

**Injaculation** — Taoist term for blocking ejaculation via perineal pressure at orgasm, causing retrograde flow.

**Ischiocavernosus** — Pelvic floor muscle compressing the roots of the penis; increases rigidity.

**Kegel** — Voluntary contraction of the pelvic floor muscles.

**Levator ani** — The deep pelvic floor muscle group including the pubococcygeus.

**Lymphocele** — Firm, cord-like, painless swelling behind the corona from a temporarily blocked lymph vessel after vigorous activity. Resolves.

**Meatus** — The urethral opening at the tip.

**Milking** — Expressing prostatic fluid by prostate massage; also a single-direction stroking technique.

**Mindful masturbation** — Solo practice with attention on sensation, without a goal.

**Moral incongruence** — Conflict between one's sexual behavior and one's moral beliefs about it; the strongest predictor of distress about pornography use.

**Myotonia** — Involuntary muscle tension during arousal.

**NEO (non-ejaculatory orgasm)** — Orgasm without ejaculation; typically little or no refractory period.

**Nitric oxide (NO)** — The signaling molecule released by parasympathetic nerves to initiate erection.

**NoFap** — Online community and practice of abstaining from pornography and/or masturbation.

**Onanism** — Archaic term for masturbation, from the Genesis story of Onan.

**Osmolality** — Concentration of dissolved particles; lubricants far above body osmolality (~300 mOsm/kg) damage epithelial cells. WHO: ideal <380, maximum 1,200.

**Paraphimosis** — Foreskin trapped behind the glans and swelling. Emergency.

**PC muscle (pubococcygeus)** — Pelvic floor muscle; the usual target of "Kegel" instructions.

**PDE5 (phosphodiesterase type 5)** — Enzyme that ends erection by breaking down cGMP. Inhibited by sildenafil, tadalafil, etc.

**PE (premature ejaculation)** — Ejaculation sooner than desired with inability to delay and distress; clinically ~≤1 min (lifelong) or bothersome reduction (acquired).

**Pearly penile papules** — Small, uniform, normal bumps in rows around the corona.

**Pelvic floor** — The muscular sling at the base of the pelvis; the voluntary control panel for erection, ejaculation, and orgasm.

**Perineum** — The area between scrotum and anus; pressure here reaches the bulb of the penis and toward the prostate.

**Peyronie's disease** — Fibrous plaque in the tunica albuginea causing curvature, pain, or deformity.

**Phimosis** — Inability to retract the foreskin.

**PIED ("porn-induced erectile dysfunction")** — Popular but not clinically established term for ED attributed to pornography.

**Plateau** — Sustained high arousal before orgasm (Masters & Johnson's second phase).

**POIS (post-orgasmic illness syndrome)** — Rare condition of flu-like symptoms for days after ejaculation.

**Point of no return (PONR)** — The moment emission begins; ejaculation will follow regardless. Arousal 9.5.

**Post-coital dysphoria** — Sadness, irritability, or emptiness after orgasm.

**Pre-ejaculate** — Clear fluid from the bulbourethral glands during arousal.

**Priapism** — Erection lasting >4 hours without stimulation. Emergency.

**Prolactin** — Hormone surging after orgasm; probable driver of satiety and the refractory period.

**Prostate** — Walnut-sized gland below the bladder; produces ~25% of semen; stimulable through the rectal wall; a source of orgasm.

**Prostate-induced orgasm** — Orgasm from prostate stimulation, often non-ejaculatory and described as more whole-body.

**Pudendal nerve** — Carries sensation from the genitals and motor commands to the pelvic floor.

**Raphe** — The midline seam running from the frenulum down the underside of the penis, scrotum, and perineum.

**Refractory period** — Post-ejaculation period during which further orgasm is difficult or impossible.

**Retrograde ejaculation** — Semen entering the bladder instead of exiting; from medications, diabetes, surgery, or deliberate blocking.

**Reverse Kegel** — Deliberate relaxation and gentle downward release of the pelvic floor.

**Ridged band** — Specialized sensory tissue just inside the tip of the foreskin.

**Ruined orgasm** — Stimulation stopped at or just after the PONR; ejaculation with muted or absent orgasmic sensation.

**Semen retention** — Practice of avoiding ejaculation based on the belief that semen contains vital essence. Not supported physiologically.

**Seminal vesicles** — Paired glands producing ~65% of semen volume.

**Sensate focus** — Masters & Johnson's therapy technique of goal-free touch with attention on sensation.

**Sextortion** — Blackmail using intimate images.

**Smegma** — Normal accumulation of shed cells and oils under the foreskin.

**Sounding** — Inserting rods into the urethra. Significant injury risk.

**Spectatoring** — Self-monitoring during sex; inhibits arousal and orgasm.

**Squeeze technique** — Masters & Johnson's PE method: firm squeeze below the glans at high arousal.

**Stop-start technique** — Semans' PE method: stop stimulation at high arousal, resume when it subsides.

**Supernormal stimulus** — A stimulus engineered beyond anything in nature (e.g., internet pornography), which may shift reference points.

**Sympathetic / parasympathetic** — The two branches of the autonomic nervous system. Parasympathetic drives erection; sympathetic drives emission/ejaculation.

**Tantra / Taoist sexual practice** — Traditions using breath, attention, and (in Taoism) semen retention for extended sexual experience.

**Teledildonics** — App-controlled or remote-partner sex toys.

**Testosterone** — Primary male sex hormone; produced mainly during sleep; unaffected by masturbation frequency.

**TPE/TPR** — Thermoplastic elastomer/rubber; porous, non-toxic material of most strokers.

**Tunica albuginea** — Fibrous sheath around the erectile bodies (and around each testis). Tears in penile fracture.

**Vas deferens** — Tube carrying sperm from the epididymis toward the urethra.

**Veno-occlusion** — Compression of draining veins during erection that traps blood.

**Whole-body (full-body) orgasm** — Orgasmic sensation spreading beyond the pelvis.
