# Chapter 17 — Life Stages & Circumstances

Solo sexuality changes across a lifetime and across circumstances. What's optimal at 22 isn't at 62; what works living alone doesn't in a shared apartment; a long-distance relationship, a new baby, a chronic illness, a disability, or a surgery each change the picture. This chapter covers the major contexts.

## 17.1 Young adulthood (18–29)

**Physiology.** Peak testosterone, shortest refractory periods, fastest arousal, most frequent spontaneous erections, highest average frequency of masturbation. Also: the least control — arousal comes fast, and the reflex is hair-trigger.

**Common patterns and their consequences.**

- **The adolescent habit persists.** Most men reach adulthood with a technique learned for speed and secrecy at 13–15 — fast, tight, often dry, often prone or hunched, often to visual material. This is the age at which that habit either gets examined or gets locked in for decades. Chapters 6, 8, 9 are the intervention.
- **High frequency is normal.** Daily or more is common and isn't a problem by itself. The 15.1 questions are the test.
- **Screen-dependence is most likely to have formed here.** Men who came of age with unlimited streaming pornography are the population where conditioning concerns (Chapter 14) are most relevant. A screen-free session per week is good range maintenance.
- **Partnered sex often starts here**, and the gap between solo habit and partnered reality shows up as PE (from rushing), situational ED (from performance anxiety or conditioning), or DE (from idiosyncratic technique). The fix is solo practice that resembles partnered conditions (lubricated, moderate, varied).
- **Shared housing.** Privacy is the practical constraint. Headphones, timing, a locked door, quick cleanup, and a stash for supplies. Shower sessions are a standard solution (silicone lube is waterproof).

**Priorities.** Build range (Chapter 6 inventory). Learn arousal control (Chapter 9) — it's easiest to learn young, and it pays off for life. Establish the lube habit. Get the screen relationship deliberate.

## 17.2 Adulthood (30–49)

**Physiology.** Gradual testosterone decline (~1%/year after 30 on average — barely noticeable in most men), refractory periods lengthening to 30 minutes–several hours, erections still reliable but more affected by fatigue, alcohol, stress. Spontaneous erections decrease; erections are more dependent on direct stimulation. Latency to orgasm typically increases somewhat — which is often experienced as *improved* control.

**Context.** Careers, relationships, often children. Time and privacy become scarce. Fatigue becomes the dominant limiting factor.

**Common patterns.**

- **The "quickie only" trap.** With less time, every session becomes a 5-minute stress release. Pleasure and skill both atrophy. The counter: one deliberate, longer session per week or two, scheduled if necessary.
- **Masturbation in a relationship** — normal and common (Chapter 15.7). Desire discrepancy is the most common sexual issue in couples; solo sex is the healthiest management tool. Secrecy causes more problems than the behavior.
- **New parents.** Sleep deprivation and total loss of privacy. Libido often drops (both partners); masturbation may be the only sexual outlet for months. This is normal. Shower sessions; sessions when the baby sleeps; not feeling guilty about it.
- **Erection quality changes** become noticeable in some men. If they coincide with weight gain, inactivity, or poor sleep, the intervention is cardiovascular health (Chapter 18). This is the decade where ED begins to correlate with heart health.
- **This is the age most men first notice prostate-related sensations** or become curious about prostate stimulation. It's a good time — the prostate is fully developed, and pelvic awareness is often better than in youth.

**Priorities.** Protect one quality session a week. Cardiovascular fitness. Pelvic floor training (Chapter 10) — it's preventive for both erectile and urinary function. Talk to your partner about solo sex.

## 17.3 Midlife and beyond (50+)

**Physiology.** More noticeable changes, with enormous individual variation:

- Erections need more direct stimulation and may be less rigid; the angle drops. Fully firm erections remain achievable for most men, especially with good vascular health.
- Refractory period: hours to a day or more.
- Ejaculate volume decreases; force decreases (pelvic floor training helps).
- Orgasm may feel less intense or more diffuse. Some men find it *more* satisfying — less urgent, more whole-body.
- Latency increases; DE becomes more common.
- Penile sensitivity declines gradually — more and more varied stimulation compensates.
- Testosterone continues declining; clinically low T affects a minority and is worth checking if there are symptoms (low libido, fatigue, low mood, loss of morning erections, muscle loss).
- The prostate enlarges (BPH) in most men, causing urinary symptoms. Alpha-blockers for this cause retrograde or reduced ejaculation. 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride) can reduce libido and cause ED in some men.
- Cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and their medications become the dominant cause of erectile problems.

**Adaptations.**

- **More time, more stimulation, more variety.** Older men need a longer build-up and benefit most from Chapter 11's principles: multiple zones, vibration (very useful as sensitivity declines), temperature, prostate stimulation.
- **Erection is not required for orgasm.** Men who accept softer erections and continue with pleasure-focused stimulation often orgasm satisfyingly. Men who fixate on rigidity often lose the erection to anxiety.
- **Vibration.** Medical-grade and consumer vibrators are widely recommended for older men and men with neuropathy or post-surgical changes because they deliver stimulation that hands can't.
- **Pelvic floor training** — the evidence for erectile and continence benefits is strongest in this age group.
- **PDE5 inhibitors** — safe and effective for most men; daily low-dose tadalafil is commonly used and also helps BPH symptoms. Discuss with a doctor, especially regarding cardiac medications.
- **Regular sexual activity, including solo, is associated with preserved erectile function** ("use it or lose it") [Koskimäki 2008]. Erections oxygenate penile tissue; long periods without them are associated with fibrosis. This is the rationale for penile rehabilitation after prostatectomy.
- **Prostate stimulation** may be especially rewarding in this age group — the prostate is larger and easier to locate — and prostate orgasm doesn't depend on erection. Men with BPH should check with a urologist first; men with prostatitis may find it helps or hurts.
- **Lube becomes more important** — skin is thinner and drier.
- **Morning is often the best time** — testosterone and energy peak.

**Widowed, divorced, or single later in life.** Solo sex may become the primary sexual outlet after decades of partnered sex. Many men in this situation have never developed a solo practice beyond the functional and find the material in this guide new. It's a legitimate and valuable sexuality in its own right, not a consolation prize.

## 17.4 After prostate surgery or treatment

Radical prostatectomy (for cancer) removes the prostate and seminal vesicles: orgasm remains possible (often after a recovery period) but is **dry** — no ejaculate. Erectile function is frequently impaired, sometimes permanently, depending on nerve preservation. Radiation, hormone therapy, and BPH procedures have varying effects.

- **Orgasm without ejaculation** after prostatectomy is well-documented and for many men remains satisfying, sometimes altered in quality (more diffuse, sometimes with urine leakage at orgasm — "climacturia" — which is manageable).
- **Penile rehabilitation** — regular erections via PDE5 inhibitors, vacuum device, or injections, starting soon after surgery — is standard urological advice to preserve tissue and maximize recovery of natural erections.
- **Masturbation is encouraged** as part of rehabilitation by most urologists.
- **Vibration** is particularly helpful for reaching orgasm with reduced sensation or without erection.
- **The prostate is gone**, so prostate stimulation as such isn't available; some men find perineal and anal stimulation still pleasurable.
- Sexual medicine specialists and pelvic floor physiotherapists are the resources.

## 17.5 Disability and chronic illness

**Spinal cord injury.** Depending on level and completeness: reflexogenic erections often preserved with touch even when psychogenic erections are lost; orgasm may be experienced differently (often described as diffuse, sometimes non-genital); ejaculation frequently impaired. Vibratory stimulation (medical-grade, high-amplitude) is used clinically to induce ejaculation. Many men with SCI develop new erogenous zones at or above the injury level. Specialist sexual rehabilitation exists.

**Diabetes.** Neuropathy reduces sensation; vascular disease impairs erection; retrograde ejaculation is possible. Tight glucose control preserves function. Vibration compensates for reduced sensation. PDE5 inhibitors are effective for many.

**Multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's, and other neurological conditions.** Variable sexual effects. Timing sessions to medication and energy peaks; vibration; positioning aids; PDE5 inhibitors.

**Chronic pain.** Orgasm raises pain thresholds and releases endorphins — many chronic pain patients find masturbation genuinely analgesic. Positioning and pacing matter; heat before a session; short sessions when energy is low.

**Cardiac disease.** Sexual activity is safe for most cardiac patients; the exertion of masturbation is roughly equivalent to climbing two flights of stairs. Ask the cardiologist; the answer is almost always yes. Nitrate medications preclude PDE5 inhibitors.

**Mobility limitations.** Toys and devices become essential rather than optional — automatic strokers, hands-free vibrators, mounted devices, long-handled tools. Positioning cushions. Occupational therapists can advise on adaptations.

**Mental illness and its medications.** SSRIs commonly cause DE, reduced libido, and reduced sensation (Chapter 16). Antipsychotics can cause ED and reduced libido. Depression itself reduces desire. Discuss with the prescriber — dose, timing, switching, and adjunct medications can help. Masturbation is often one of the few reliable pleasures during depressive episodes and shouldn't be an additional source of guilt.

**Chronic illness generally.** Solo sex is often more accessible than partnered sex during illness — less energy, no performance concern, full control of pacing. It's a legitimate part of coping and quality of life.

## 17.6 Relationships

**Solo sex while partnered.** Normal, common, healthy. It manages desire discrepancy, maintains self-knowledge, provides an outlet during illness/pregnancy/travel/conflict, and is simply a different pleasure. Most sex therapists consider mutual acceptance of solo sex a marker of a healthy sexual relationship.

**Disclosure.** Openness is protective; secrecy is corrosive. A simple conversation — "I masturbate, probably X times a week, sometimes with porn/whatever; it's not about you; how do you feel about that?" — prevents most problems. Partners' feelings vary; hearing them out without defensiveness is the skill.

**Mutual masturbation** — masturbating together, watching each other, or each other's hands — is an underused practice that transfers solo skill to the partnership, teaches each partner what the other likes, and is intensely intimate for many couples.

**When a partner objects.** Explore why: insecurity ("am I not enough?"), moral belief, feeling deceived, or a specific concern (porn content, frequency displacing partnered sex). Each has a different response. Total prohibition of solo sex is an unusual demand; concerns about porn are common and negotiable.

**Long-distance.** Solo sex is the primary outlet, often synchronized with a partner (video, phone, teledildonic devices — see Chapter 20 for privacy). Shared masturbation across distance is intimacy, not a substitute for it.

**During pregnancy and postpartum.** Partner libido and availability vary; solo sex fills the gap without pressure. Normal and healthy.

**After a breakup or bereavement.** Solo sex may feel empty, may be a comfort, may bring emotional release (post-orgasm crying is common and healthy in grief). All normal.

## 17.7 Trans men and non-binary people with penises

For readers who've had phalloplasty or metoidioplasty, or who have a penis and identify as trans or non-binary:

- **Metoidioplasty** (releasing a testosterone-enlarged clitoris): highly sensitive, generally 3–8 cm, capable of erection (usually not penetrative), and orgasmic. Technique resembles clitoral stimulation more than penile — focused, lubricated, often lighter — with the anatomy chapter's frenulum/glans principles applying.
- **Phalloplasty**: sensation depends on nerve hookup and healing, typically developing over 1–2 years; the buried clitoris is often the main orgasmic source, stimulated through the base of the phallus; erection requires an implant or external device. Sex therapists and surgeons specializing in gender-affirming care are the resource.
- **Testosterone** for trans men on HRT increases libido, often dramatically, and enlarges the clitoris; many trans men report masturbation frequency increasing substantially after starting T.
- **Dysphoria** can complicate solo sex; approaches that focus on sensation rather than visual/anatomical attention (eyes closed, mindfulness) help many.
- The pelvic floor, prostate-adjacent (for those without prostates, the anterior vaginal wall / Skene's gland area is analogous), and erogenous zone material is broadly applicable.

For trans women and non-binary people assigned male at birth, on estrogen and/or anti-androgens: libido and erectile function typically decrease; sensation changes; orgasm often becomes more diffuse and whole-body; ejaculate reduces or stops. Masturbation approaches shift toward lighter touch, non-penile zones, and (for many) the prostate. This guide's pleasure-maximization and mindfulness chapters apply; the erection-focused material less so.

## 17.8 Circumstances

**No privacy** (dorm, barracks, shared room, living with parents as an adult). Shower sessions with silicone lube; timing around others' schedules; noise management (headphones, white noise, bedding); quick cleanup kit; discretion with toys and supplies (a locked box). Most men in this situation default to fast, silent, screen-based sessions — the mindful and slow approaches are harder but not impossible (a 15-minute quiet session is achievable).

**Travel.** Hotel sessions are often the best of the month — privacy, no obligations, a different environment. Bring lube (travel size, in checked luggage or under liquid limits). Toys in carry-on are routine and TSA doesn't care.

**Religious communities and moral conflict.** Chapter 19 covers the traditions. Practically: moral incongruence (Chapter 14, 15) is the source of most distress; resolving it — through study, a thoughtful advisor, or therapy — matters more than changing the behavior.

**Incarceration, institutional living.** Solo sex is typically the only sexual outlet; privacy is minimal; rules vary. Beyond the scope of this guide except to say it's normal and necessary.

**Military deployment, remote work.** Extended periods away from partners. Solo sex maintains sexual function and provides stress relief. Long-distance intimacy tools (Chapter 20 for security).

**Athletic training.** The "sex before competition" myth is unsupported — masturbation the night before doesn't impair performance [Stefani 2016 review]. Immediately before (within an hour) may reduce aggression/arousal slightly in some men; individual. Post-workout sessions are often excellent (Chapter 11).

### Key takeaways

- **20s**: build range, learn control, establish lube, make the screen relationship deliberate. Easiest time to learn skills that last.
- **30s–40s**: protect one quality session a week; cardiovascular health; pelvic floor; talk to your partner. ED begins to track heart health.
- **50+**: more time, more zones, vibration, prostate; erection isn't required for orgasm; PDE5i are safe for most; "use it or lose it" is real.
- **After prostate surgery**: dry orgasm is real and satisfying; penile rehabilitation matters; vibration helps.
- **Disability and illness**: solo sex is often the most accessible sexuality; toys become essential; vibration compensates for reduced sensation; medication effects are negotiable with prescribers.
- **Relationships**: solo sex while partnered is normal and healthy; secrecy is the problem; mutual masturbation transfers skill.
- **Trans and non-binary readers**: anatomy differs; sensation principles apply; specialist resources exist.
- **No privacy**: shower, timing, headphones, locked box. Quality is still possible in 15 quiet minutes.
