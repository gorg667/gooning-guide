# Chapter 5 — Hygiene, Skin Care & Injury Prevention

Everything in the later chapters assumes a healthy, comfortable body. This chapter covers daily care, the injuries that actually happen, and how to avoid them. Most of it is common sense once the anatomy is understood, but the details matter.

## 5.1 Daily genital hygiene

**Washing.** Once daily with warm water is sufficient. Mild, fragrance-free soap or a pH-balanced wash is fine on the shaft and scrotum; on the glans and inner foreskin, water alone or a very mild cleanser is better — the glans is mucosal-adjacent tissue and harsh soaps strip its protective film, causing dryness, irritation, and paradoxically more odor.

**Uncircumcised men.** Retract the foreskin gently (never force it), rinse the glans and coronal sulcus with warm water, remove any smegma (a normal accumulation of shed skin cells and oils — not dirt, not a sign of poor hygiene unless allowed to build up for days), pat dry, and return the foreskin to its normal position. Leaving the foreskin retracted after washing can cause **paraphimosis** — the foreskin traps behind the glans and swells — which is a medical emergency if it doesn't resolve quickly. Always return it.

**Drying.** Moisture trapped under the foreskin or in the groin folds encourages yeast (*Candida*) and bacterial overgrowth. Dry thoroughly. If you sweat heavily, an antifungal/absorbent powder (cornstarch-based; avoid talc) in the groin crease can help. Cotton or moisture-wicking underwear; change daily or after exercise.

**Before a session.** Clean hands. If using toys, clean them (Chapter 13). If you plan anal stimulation, see Section 5.7.

**After a session.** Rinse or wipe away lubricant and semen. Water-based lubes wash off easily; silicone lubes need soap. Left-on silicone lube isn't harmful but is slippery and attracts lint. Dried semen on skin is harmless but can be mildly irritating over time; on the glans/foreskin it can promote smegma buildup. Urinating after ejaculation flushes the urethra and is a reasonable habit (it reduces the chance of urethral irritation from residual semen, and some urologists suggest it may reduce UTI risk, though evidence in men is thin).

## 5.2 Skin of the penis: keeping it healthy

The penile skin is thin, mobile, and has fewer oil glands than most body skin. It tolerates a lot of friction but has limits.

**Signs of skin stress:**

- Redness, especially in a band where the hand grips.
- Dryness, flaking, or shininess.
- Small cracks or fissures, often at the frenulum or coronal sulcus.
- Tenderness or a "raw" feeling the next day.
- Darkened, thickened skin at a habitual grip point (a callus-like response).

**Causes:** dry masturbation (no lubricant), excessive grip pressure, high frequency without recovery time, harsh soaps, allergic reaction to a lubricant ingredient, or friction from clothing.

**Fixes:**

1. **Use lubricant.** This is the single biggest skin-protective change most men can make, especially circumcised men. See Chapter 7.
2. **Reduce grip pressure.** If you're leaving red marks, you're gripping too hard. See Chapter 6.
3. **Give skin recovery time.** If the skin is irritated, 24–48 hours off (or a session with much lighter, well-lubricated touch) lets it repair.
4. **Moisturize.** Plain, fragrance-free moisturizer (or a barrier ointment like petroleum jelly or a zinc-oxide cream for cracked spots) on the shaft after washing. Not on the glans/inner foreskin unless specifically formulated.
5. **Check your products.** If irritation started with a new lube, condom, soap, or laundry detergent, that's the likely culprit. Glycerin, propylene glycol, parabens, fragrances, warming/cooling agents, and spermicides (nonoxynol-9) are common irritants.

**Foreskin-specific:**

- **Phimosis** — inability to retract the foreskin over the glans. Common in boys; usually resolves by adolescence. In adults, mild cases often respond to gentle daily stretching plus a prescribed steroid cream (typically betamethasone 0.05% for 4–8 weeks) [Zavras 2009]; severe or scarred cases may need a minor procedure. Never force retraction — tearing causes scarring and worsens it.
- **Frenulum breve** — a short frenulum that pulls the glans downward when erect, causing discomfort or tearing. A urologist can perform a frenuloplasty (minor, often outpatient). Small frenulum tears during sex or masturbation are common, bleed briefly, and usually heal in a week; avoid stretching it during healing, keep it clean, and use lube afterward.
- **Balanitis** — inflammation of the glans (redness, itching, discharge, odor). Usually yeast or bacterial; often triggered by moisture, soap, or poorly controlled blood sugar. Treated with antifungal or antibiotic cream. Recurrent balanitis is a reason to check for diabetes.

## 5.3 Injuries: what actually happens

### 5.3.1 Friction burns and skin tears — common, minor

Dry, fast, or prolonged stroking. Symptoms: raw, red, sometimes weeping skin; stinging on contact with urine or sweat. **Treatment:** stop, keep clean and dry, apply a plain barrier ointment, wear loose underwear, abstain from friction for 2–5 days until healed. **Prevention:** lubricant, lighter grip, shorter sessions or breaks.

### 5.3.2 Frenulum tears — common, minor to moderate

The frenulum is under tension during erection and is the first thing to tear if the foreskin or skin is pulled too hard downward. Bleeds noticeably (it's vascular) but stops with gentle pressure. Heals in 1–2 weeks. Repeated tears cause scarring and a tighter frenulum — a vicious cycle. **Prevention:** lube, don't pull the skin forcefully toward the base at full erection, address frenulum breve if present.

### 5.3.3 Bruising and lymphatic swelling — uncommon, minor

A very tight grip or aggressive edging session can cause a bruise on the shaft or a **lymphocele** — a firm, painless, cord-like swelling just behind the corona, from a temporarily blocked lymphatic vessel. Alarming to look at but harmless; resolves in days to a couple of weeks with rest. **Prevention:** moderate grip, don't use hard constriction (rings, hands) for extended periods.

### 5.3.4 Penile fracture — rare, serious

Rupture of the tunica albuginea of a corpus cavernosum, from forceful bending of the erect penis. Classic scenario: partnered sex with the penis slipping and hitting the perineum or pubic bone; less commonly, aggressive masturbation, rolling onto an erection, or "taqaandan" (deliberately bending the erect penis to make it crack, a practice documented in some regions). Signs: a popping sound, immediate detumescence, sharp pain, rapid swelling and bruising ("eggplant deformity"). **This is a surgical emergency** — go to the ER. Untreated, it heals with curvature and erectile dysfunction. Incidence is low (perhaps 1 per 175,000 per year) but the consequence of ignoring it is permanent. **Prevention:** never bend, fold, or apply lateral force to an erect penis.

### 5.3.5 Urethral injury — rare, serious

From inserting objects into the urethra ("sounding" — Chapter 12) or from extreme pressure on the underside. Signs: bleeding from the meatus, pain on urination, inability to urinate. Needs medical evaluation. Urethral strictures (scarring) can result and can permanently impair urination and ejaculation.

### 5.3.6 Foreign body and vacuum injuries — rare, potentially serious

Objects that don't belong (bottles, vacuum cleaner attachments, rigid tubes) cause a disproportionate share of masturbation-related ER visits. Constriction injuries from rings that can't be removed cause ischemia within hours. **Rules:** only use things designed for the purpose, never use household vacuums, never use rigid rings that don't stretch or have a quick release, remove any ring within 20–30 minutes or at the first sign of pain, coldness, or color change.

### 5.3.7 Pudendal nerve irritation — uncommon, usually reversible

Numbness, tingling, or reduced sensation in the penis, scrotum, or perineum. Causes: prolonged pressure on the perineum (cycling, hard seats, prone masturbation on a hard surface), very tight grip compressing the dorsal nerve, or vibrator overuse. **Treatment:** remove the cause; sensation typically returns over days to weeks. Persistent numbness needs evaluation.

### 5.3.8 Peyronie's disease — not caused by masturbation per se

Fibrous plaque formation in the tunica albuginea, causing curvature, indentation, pain, or shortening. Affects perhaps 3–9% of men, more with age. The cause is thought to be repeated micro-trauma to the erect penis in genetically susceptible men — so *forceful* sex or masturbation with buckling may contribute, but ordinary masturbation does not. **Signs:** new curvature, a palpable hard lump along the shaft, pain with erection, hourglass narrowing. **Action:** see a urologist early — treatment (traction, collagenase injections, surgery) is more effective in the acute phase.

## 5.4 The "death grip": a special case

The term (popularized by columnist Dan Savage) describes habitual masturbation with a very tight grip, often fast, often dry. It's not a medical diagnosis. The concerns are:

1. **Skin damage** — covered above.
2. **Conditioning** — learning to need that specific intense stimulation to orgasm, leading to difficulty with a partner or with gentler touch. This is the real and well-documented issue [Perelman 2016]. It is not nerve damage; it's a learned threshold, and it's reversible.
3. **Possible transient sensitivity reduction** — from compressed nerves and skin thickening; also reversible.

**Fix:** lube, loose grip, slower speed, variety of technique, and if needed, a structured retraining period (Chapter 16). Most men notice a substantial change within 2–4 weeks.

## 5.5 Posture, ergonomics, and repetitive strain

Masturbation is a repetitive physical activity. Over decades it can produce:

- **Wrist and forearm strain** from repeated fast stroking, especially with a tight grip. Alternate hands, vary grip, use sleeves/toys for long sessions, and stretch.
- **Lower back and neck pain** from hunching over a screen or awkward positions. Lie down, use pillows, put the screen at eye level or skip it.
- **Perineal pressure** from prone (face-down) masturbation on a hard surface — see 5.3.7. Prone masturbation, where the man rubs against a mattress or pillow, is also a known contributor to delayed ejaculation because the stimulation pattern is hard to replicate any other way [Sank 1998]. If it's your only method, diversifying is worthwhile.
- **Eye strain** from screens in the dark. Obvious fix.

## 5.6 Frequency, recovery, and skin

Skin, like muscle, adapts with recovery. If you masturbate daily with lube and moderate grip, your skin will be fine indefinitely. If you masturbate multiple times a day, or for hours, or dry, the skin doesn't get recovery time and problems accumulate. **Rule of thumb:** if there's any redness, rawness, or tenderness the next day, that's a signal to add lube, lighten grip, or take a day off.

## 5.7 Anal hygiene and safety (for prostate/anal stimulation)

Anal play is covered in Chapter 12; the safety essentials are here because they're prerequisites.

- **The rectum is not a sterile environment**, but it also normally contains little stool beyond the reach of a finger or small toy — stool is stored higher, in the sigmoid colon. A bowel movement an hour or more before, and washing the external area, is sufficient for most play. **Douching/enemas are optional**, not required, and frequent douching irritates the rectal lining and disrupts its flora. If you do douche, use plain lukewarm water in a small bulb (not a shower attachment at pressure), once, gently.
- **Lubricant is mandatory.** The rectum does not self-lubricate. Use plenty — much more than you'd use on the penis. Water-based or silicone; avoid numbing lubes (pain is information you need). See Chapter 7 on osmolality — hyperosmolar lubes damage rectal epithelium [Ayehunie 2017; WHO 2012].
- **Nothing without a flared base or retrieval handle goes inside.** The rectum can draw objects upward via peristalsis; retrieval sometimes requires surgery. This is not rare in ER data.
- **Trimmed, smooth fingernails**, or use a glove/finger cot.
- **Go slowly.** The external sphincter needs a minute or more to relax. Pain means stop.
- **Wash anything that has been in the anus before it touches the penis or urethra** — introducing gut bacteria into the urethra causes urethritis/UTI.
- **Bleeding.** Minor spotting from a small fissure can happen; stop for a few days. Persistent or heavy bleeding — see a doctor.
- **Hemorrhoids and fissures** are aggravated by anal play; let them heal fully first.

## 5.8 Testicular self-examination

Build this into one session a month:

1. Best done when warm (after a shower or during arousal, when the scrotum is relaxed).
2. Roll each testis gently between thumb and fingers.
3. Feel for: hard lumps (especially painless ones), a change in size or firmness, a heavy or aching sensation.
4. The epididymis (soft, ropey structure on the back/top) is normal — learn what yours feels like so you don't mistake it for a lump.
5. Anything new or persistent → see a doctor within days, not weeks. Testicular cancer is >95% curable when caught early.

**Also normal:** one testis lower and slightly larger; a soft, fluid-like swelling that's been stable for years (likely a hydrocele or spermatocele — benign but worth a one-time check); veins that feel like a "bag of worms" (varicocele — common, usually harmless, occasionally affects fertility).

## 5.9 When to see a doctor

**Urgent (same day / ER):**

- Popping sound + pain + rapid swelling of an erect penis (fracture).
- Erection lasting more than 4 hours (priapism — causes permanent damage).
- Foreskin stuck retracted and swelling (paraphimosis).
- Object stuck in the rectum or urethra.
- Ring or constriction device that can't be removed.
- Severe testicular pain of sudden onset (torsion — a few-hour window to save the testis).

**Soon (days):**

- Blood in semen or urine (usually benign, but check).
- Any testicular lump.
- New penile curvature or a hard plaque.
- Sores, blisters, warts, or ulcers on the genitals.
- Persistent numbness.
- Painful urination or discharge.
- Persistent pelvic or perineal pain.

**Routine:**

- Persistent erectile difficulty (also a cardiovascular warning sign — see Chapter 18).
- Ejaculation problems (premature, delayed, absent, painful).
- Post-orgasm illness symptoms.
- Feeling out of control of your sexual behavior.

---

### Key takeaways

- **Daily warm-water wash**, gentle on the glans, dry thoroughly, return the foreskin. Rinse off lube and semen afterward.
- **Lubricant + moderate grip** prevents nearly all common skin problems.
- Skin needs **recovery time**; redness/tenderness the next day means adjust.
- **Frenulum tears, friction burns, and lymphoceles** are common and heal with rest. **Penile fracture, urethral injury, and stuck constriction devices** are emergencies.
- **Never bend an erect penis forcefully.** Never insert household objects. Never use a ring without a quick release or stretch.
- **Anal play:** lots of lube, flared base, go slow, wash before touching the penis.
- **Monthly testicular self-exam.** New lumps → doctor within days.
- **"Death grip"** is a learned threshold, not nerve damage; it's fixable.
