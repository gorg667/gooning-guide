# Research notes (sources verified via search; use in chapters + references)

## Prostate cancer & ejaculation frequency
- Rider JR et al. 2016, *European Urology* 70(6):974-982. Health Professionals Follow-up Study,
  ~32,000 men, 18 yr follow-up. ≥21 ejaculations/month vs 4–7/month: ~20% lower risk of PCa
  diagnosis (HR ≈0.80 at ages 20–29; ≈0.78 at 40–49). Observational; Mayo Clinic notes "no
  conclusive evidence". Cite as "association, not proof". PMID 27033442.

## Testosterone & abstinence
- Jiang M et al. 2003 (J Zhejiang Univ Sci) – small (n=28), reported T peak ~day 7 of abstinence
  (~145% of baseline) then return to baseline. NOTE: paper has been flagged/retracted per
  ResearchGate listing ("RETRACTED ARTICLE"). Treat as weak.
- Exton MS et al. 2001 – 3-week abstinence, slightly higher T; masturbation → transient
  changes only.
- Isenmann E et al. 2021 (PMC8697462) – hormonal response after masturbation in young healthy
  men: no meaningful sustained T change.
- Conclusion: masturbation does not lower baseline testosterone; short-term fluctuations are
  small and clinically meaningless. NoFap T claims not supported.

## Compulsive sexual behaviour disorder
- ICD-11 code 6C72 (impulse control disorder). Kraus SW et al. 2018 World Psychiatry
  (PMC5775124). Criteria: persistent failure to control intense repetitive sexual
  impulses → repetitive sexual behaviour; central focus of life, neglect of health/duties;
  unsuccessful efforts to reduce; continues despite little/no satisfaction or adverse
  consequences; ≥6 months; marked distress/impairment. EXCLUSION: distress entirely from
  moral judgement/disapproval does NOT qualify. Gola M et al. 2020 (PMC9295236).
- Bőthe B et al. 2024 Addiction – problematic pornography use across 42 countries; PPU
  common manifestation of CSBD; prevalence estimates ~3-10% depending on cutoff.

## Pelvic floor & PE
- Pastore AL et al. 2014, Ther Adv Urol (PMC4003840): 40 men lifelong PE (IELT ≤1 min),
  12-week PFM rehab; 33/40 (82.5%) gained control; IELT increased from ~31.7s to ~146s.
  Follow-up (2018, 5-yr experience): ~54% durable cure.
- Kegels for men: contract muscles used to stop urine / lift testicles; 10 reps x 3/day,
  progressive holds. Hypertonic pelvic floor is real; reverse Kegels / relaxation matter.

## Lubricant safety
- WHO 2012 advisory note (Use and procurement of additional lubricants for male and female
  condoms): osmolality ideally <380 mOsm/kg; interim maximum 1200 mOsm/kg; pH ~4.5 vaginal /
  5.5–7 rectal; avoid high glycerin/propylene glycol concentrations, nonoxynol-9,
  chlorhexidine, polyquaternium-15 (concerns). Ayehunie S et al. 2017 (PMC5977164) –
  hyperosmolal lubricants reduce epithelial barrier. Many commercial lubes are 2000–6000
  mOsm/kg. Iso-osmotic brands exist.
- Oil-based lubes degrade latex condoms; silicone lube degrades silicone toys (contested but
  standard advice); water-based safe with everything.

## Pornography & ED
- Grubbs JB & Gola M 2019, J Sex Med (PMID 30621919): 3 samples; little/no association between
  mere porn use & ED; association between *self-perceived problematic* use & ED
  (cross-sectional). Prause & Pfaus 2015: more porn viewing → *higher* arousal responsiveness.
- Jacobs T et al. 2021 (PMC8569536), Belgian/Danish survey n≈3400: problematic porn use
  (CYPAT) associated with ED; masturbation frequency itself not; longitudinal causality
  unclear.
- Rowland DL et al. 2024 Curr Sex Health Rep: mixed; some studies show improved function.
- Balanced conclusion: "porn-induced ED" as a distinct diagnosis is not established;
  problematic/compulsive use, conditioning to specific stimuli, and performance anxiety are
  plausible contributors for some men; practical: vary stimuli, reduce reliance, address
  anxiety.

## Delayed ejaculation & masturbation style
- Perelman MA 2005, 2016 (PMC5001992): "idiosyncratic masturbatory style" — high speed,
  pressure, intensity, or duration not replicable by partner; + high frequency (>3x/wk) +
  disparity between fantasy and reality → DE. Treatment: masturbation retraining,
  "suspend masturbation" periods, varying style, fantasy alignment. "Death grip" is lay term
  (coined by Dan Savage) for this.

## POIS
- Nguyen HMT et al. 2018 Sex Med Rev (PMID 29128269): ~50 cases in literature; flu-like
  symptoms, cognitive fog, 2–7 days post-orgasm; onset minutes–hours; pathophysiology
  unknown (autoimmune/allergy to own semen hypothesis, others). Ponce S et al. 2025 J Sex Med
  systematic review of treatments. Rare; know it exists; see a doctor.

## Prevalence data
- Herbenick D et al. 2010 J Sex Med (NSSHB, n=5865, ages 14–94): solo masturbation in past
  month 27.9%–68.6% of men depending on age group; lifetime ~80%+ men (higher in later
  surveys). Herbenick 2023 Arch Sex Behav (2018 NSSHB): 35.9% of men reported masturbating
  ≥ weekly-ish (vs 8.8% women; exact metric: "a few times per week or more").
- Herbenick 2021: decreases in solo masturbation frequency in adolescents & young adults
  2009→2018 (mirroring decline in partnered sex).

## Prostate orgasm
- Levin RJ 2018 Clin Anat 31(1):81-85 (PMID 29265651): prostate-induced orgasms — described
  as more intense/whole-body, may not require ejaculation, mechanism poorly understood
  (rewiring/learning proposed; anecdotal >> controlled data). ISSM Q&A 2018.

## Toy materials
- Silicone (platinum-cure), borosilicate glass, stainless steel, ABS plastic = non-porous,
  body-safe. Jelly/PVC/rubber often contain phthalates (Danish EPA 2006 study found
  phthalates + other compounds in toys); TPE/TPR = porous, non-toxic but can't be
  disinfected; replace regularly, use condom over. "Flame test" is a folk test, not
  reliable. Strokers (e.g., Fleshlight "SuperSkin") are TPE-type → porous → dry fully,
  cornstarch (not talc) powder, replace when degraded.
